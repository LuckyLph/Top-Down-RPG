# Scene generation

An editor tool that fills an area scene with generated content: terrain layers (plain data tilemaps drawn by the area's blended terrain, see [TerrainBlending.md](TerrainBlending.md)), props and mob spawn points, plus the party's start spawn. The result is saved in the scene like hand-made content. There are three generators: the forest Clearing (`Area_Clearing.unity` was made with it), the Village Forest, which joins a village, a clearing and a forest in one larger area (`Area_VillageForest.unity`), and the Lakeside, which shows most terrain types around a lake (`Area_Lakeside.unity`). All three were regenerated in Phase 3 of TerrainTypes.md, and the Lakeside again in Phase 4 for its shallows, so each scene holds exactly its generator's layout for seed 1.

## Using it

1. Open the area scene and make it the active scene. A new area starts from the area template (File > New Scene, `AreaTemplate`), whose grid already holds a ground layer for every material of the terrain style's layer order (ten today), empty, so it can be painted with anything, and everything else the writer expects. Generate or Convert Ground then trims it to the generator's layers (see below).
2. Open **Tools > TopDownRPG > World > Scene Generator** and pick a generator asset: [Generator_Clearing.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_Clearing.asset), [Generator_VillageForest.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_VillageForest.asset) or [Generator_Lakeside.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_Lakeside.asset).
3. Tune its settings in the window. A map of the layout redraws on every change, one pixel per cell: each terrain layer in its material's base colour (a shade darker when a layer below it has the same colour), blocking footprints dark green, walk-through props light green, mob spawns red, the party spawn cyan. The line under it counts cells, props and mobs, and warnings say what did not fit. When the generator's layers cannot be set up (no terrain style, more than 16 layers, a layer missing its tilemap name, tile or material, or layers that are not a subsequence of the style's layer order), an error box says why and Generate and Convert Ground are disabled.
4. **Random Seed** picks a new seed. The seed is stored on the generator asset, and the same seed and settings always give the same layout.
5. **Generate** asks first, then replaces the scene's content as a single undo step. Review the scene and save it.
6. **Convert Ground** asks first, then sets the scene's ground layers up as the generator's layers without generating anything (see `GroundLayerMigration` below), as a single undo step: every painted cell stays, and props, mobs and walls are left alone. Use it after changing a generator's terrain layers (a tile, a material, a layer added or dropped) on an area you do not want to regenerate: it creates the layers the area lacks, empty, keeps a painted layer the generator no longer lists (with a warning) and removes an empty one. Phase 4 of TerrainBlending.md converted every area's dual-grid ground with it, Phase 5 gave the Clearing and the Village Forest their empty sand, mud and water layers, and Phase 2 of TerrainTypes.md trimmed both to the layers they use.

Terrain a generator lays after its props and mobs (the Clearing's campsite, the Village Forest's dirt, the Lakeside's dry grass and shallow ponds) draws from a second random stream seeded from the same seed (`AreaLayoutBuilder.StartDetailStream`), so adding such a step left every path, prop and mob of the earlier layouts where it was.

What Generate replaces:
- Every terrain layer the generator lists: the data tilemap child of the area's `Grid` with that name is cleared and filled with the layer's plain tile in exactly the layout's cells. A layer the scene does not have yet is created as a plain tilemap. Each layer is left a plain data tilemap: a dual-grid module, any tilemap under it (its render tilemap, even one left behind without its module) or a Tilemap Renderer still on it is removed, since every cell is repainted anyway. Its navigation source (added when missing) gets the layer's terrain type and its index among the layers drawn as priority, and the layers drawn are put first among the grid's children, in draw order.
- Any other layer the renderer drew, one the generator does not list: kept, with its cells and its material, when it is painted or the area's `AreaTerrain` lists it, at its material's place in the style's layer order (above everything, when the order does not list its material), with a warning naming it; removed when empty (its tilemap, a child of the grid, is deleted).
- The grid's `TerrainBlendRenderer`, added when missing: it draws exactly those layers, in the shared order (its draw order, and so the navigation priorities), with their materials and the generator's terrain style; past 16 layers the kept ones from the top are dropped with a warning. Entries without a tilemap are dropped. Its other settings (chunk size, sorting, look) stay.
- The fill tile of each of the generator's layers that the area's `AreaTerrain` lists: the layer's tile.
- Every child of the `Props` root, so props placed by hand there go too.
- Every `MobSpawnPoint` in the scene. The new ones go under a `MobSpawns` root, named `MobSpawn_<prefab>_<n>`.
- The position of the `start` spawn point, or of the first spawn point when none has that id. One is created when the scene has none.

The collision tilemap (walls), the rest of `AreaTerrain`, the lifetime scope, lights and any other objects are left alone. Generated layouts assume cells of one unit, like every area; the writer refuses any other grid.

## Pieces

All of it is editor-only code in `TopDownRPG.Editor`, under [Assets/Editor/World/Generation](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation).

| Type | Role |
|---|---|
| [AreaGenerator](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/AreaGenerator.cs) | Abstract `ScriptableObject`, one subclass per kind of environment. Holds the `Seed` the window uses and the `TerrainStyle` the area's terrain renderer draws with, lists the `TerrainLayers` it paints, bottom first: only the ones its areas use, at most 16, their materials a subsequence of the style's layer order (each one has a tilemap name, a plain `Tile`, a `TerrainType2D` and a `TerrainMaterial`), and `Generate(seed)` returns an `AreaLayout`. `PropScatter` is the base of the generators' scatter layers: a label, a prefab list, a count and a spacing; each generator's subclass adds which part of its area the layer fills. |
| [AreaLayout](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/AreaLayout.cs) | Plain data in one-unit cells: the generator's terrain layers (`TerrainLayers`) and the filled cells of each, by index in that list or by the layer itself (`TerrainCells`), with `IndexOf(material)` for tools and tests, since a layer's index depends on the generator; `Props` (prefab plus cell: the bottom-left cell of a blocking prop's footprint, or the cell and an offset inside it for a walk-through prop), `Mobs` (prefab plus cell), `SpawnCell`, and `Warnings`. |
| [AreaLayoutBuilder](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/AreaLayoutBuilder.cs) | Internal. The state and steps the generators share during one generation: the seeded random source, the layout of the generator's layers with the cells of the grass, gravel and stone layers it is given (the generators fill their other layers through their own layer fields), how deep each grass cell lies inside the edge, and which cells are reserved (kept free of blocking props), covered by a footprint or hold a walk-through prop. Other path layers it is given (the Village Forest's plaza) count as paths with gravel and stone (`IsPath`). Its steps: `LayGrass` (a wobbly superellipse, its largest 4-connected region and the edge distances), `WobblyShape`, `SampleSpline` and `Paint`, `PlaceSpawn`, `ReservePathClearance`, `ReserveSquare` and `ReserveDisc`, `ScatterProps` and `TryPlaceFootprint`, `RemoveProps` (the walk-through props on some cells, and optionally the blocking props covering them, their cells freed), `MobCells` and `PlaceMobs`, `ReachableFromSpawn` and `FloodFill`; `StartDetailStream` switches every later random draw to a second stream seeded from the layout's seed, for terrain laid after the props and mobs. |
| [ClearingGenerator](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/ClearingGenerator.cs) | The forest clearing, described below. Create menu: `TopDownRPG/World/Clearing Generator`. |
| [VillageForestGenerator](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/VillageForestGenerator.cs) | A village, a clearing and a forest in one area, described below. `GenerateWithRegions(seed)` also returns the region of every grass cell, which the tests read. Create menu: `TopDownRPG/World/Village Forest Generator`. |
| [LakesideGenerator](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/LakesideGenerator.cs) | A lakeside with every terrain type, described below. Create menu: `TopDownRPG/World/Lakeside Generator`. |
| [AreaLayoutWriter](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/AreaLayoutWriter.cs) | `Write(grid, generator, layout)` writes a layout into the grid's scene as described above. It refuses a layout of other layers than the generator's, and a generator whose layers cannot be set up (`AreaGroundSetup.Problem`): no terrain style, more than 16 layers, a layer lacking a tilemap name, a tile or a material, two layers of one tilemap name or one material, or layers that are not a subsequence of the style's layer order. Blocking props go to `PropGridSnapping.RootOnCells`, so their footprints cover exactly their cells, and walk-through props are snapped to the pixel grid. Mob spawns and the party spawn go to cell centres. |
| [GroundLayerMigration](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/GroundLayerMigration.cs) | `Migrate(grid, generator)` (or with a style and layers) sets the grid's ground layers up as the generator's without repainting them, as one undo step, and returns a line per change: for each terrain layer whose tilemap the grid has, every filled cell holds the layer's tile (a layer holding another tile is cleared and filled in one batch, so it keeps no record of the old tiles), its dual-grid parts and any Tilemap Renderer go, and its navigation source gets the layer's terrain type and draw order. A layer the renderer drew that the generator does not list is kept when painted or edited at runtime and removed when empty, and the renderer and `AreaTerrain`'s fill tiles are set, as Generate does. A layer whose cells already hold its tile is rewritten too when it still keeps records of tiles it held before. Terrain layers the scene lacks are created empty, first among the grid's children in the generator's order and with their navigation source, as Generate creates them; a scene with none of them is refused with an error, and a second run changes nothing. The window's Convert Ground runs it. |
| [AreaGroundSetup](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/AreaGroundSetup.cs) | Internal. The steps the writer and the migration share: checking a generator's layers and style (`Problem`, `CanSetUp`), setting the layers up (`SetUpLayers`: the generator's layers merged with the renderer's other painted or runtime-edited layers in the style's layer order, empty other layers removed, each layer created when missing and put at its place among the grid's children with its dual-grid parts removed, the navigation sources and the renderer set, recorded in an order an undo can revert through), removing a layer's dual-grid parts, setting a navigation source, and setting `AreaTerrain`'s fill tiles, each with undo and recording nothing when already set. |
| [SceneGeneratorWindow](../Assets/Editor/World/Generation/SceneGeneratorWindow.cs) | The window. It targets the active scene's grid (the one with the `NavigationGrid2D`, else the first `Grid`), draws the generator's own inspector and the preview (each layer in its material's base colour), and runs Generate or Convert Ground after the GUI pass. Both are disabled in play mode, and the writer and the migration refuse to run there, since a change to the scene during play is lost when play stops; both are disabled, with the reason in an error box, while the generator's layers cannot be set up. |

The generators work in cells and know footprints only through `PropGridSnapping.CellsCovered`, the same rule the writer and `NavigationGrid2D` follow. `AreaGenerationTests` checks that a written layout is blocked by the navigation grid on exactly its footprint cells.

## The Clearing generator

One generation, in order:

1. **Grass**: the cells whose centres lie inside a superellipse of `size` (`squareness` 2 is an ellipse, higher is squarer). Its radius wobbles by up to `edgeWobble` with four random harmonics around it. Only the largest 4-connected region is kept. Every grass cell records its distance in steps (8 neighbours) to the nearest cell without grass.
2. **Stone road**: a Catmull-Rom spline through five points from beyond the west edge to beyond the east edge, each at a random height within `roadMeander` of the middle. Every grass cell whose centre lies within half of `roadWidth` of the curve is painted. A road cell outside the grass is dropped, so paths always lie on grass.
3. **Gravel paths**: `gravelPathCount` branches leave the middle half of the road, alternating north and south, and run out past the edge through a point that bends them sideways.
4. **Spawn**: on the road where it passes `spawnAlongRoad` (from -1 for the west edge to 1 for the east edge), or at the nearest road cell that has grass all around it for `spawnClearRadius` cells. If no road cell has that room, the spawn goes to the deepest grass cell, with a warning. Both generators share this rule.
5. **Open ground**: blocking props are kept off the path cells and `pathClearance` cells beside them, which leaves a gap in the forest edge where a path leaves, and off the disc of `spawnClearRadius` around the spawn.
6. **Scatter layers**, in list order, so earlier layers take the room first. Each one tries the cells of its zone in random order: `Border` is the band `borderDepth` cells deep along the edge, `Interior` is everything inside it. Each try takes a random prefab from the layer, and stops once `count` props are placed. A prefab with a footprint collider is a blocking prop. Its footprint stands on the tried cell, as centred as its width allows, and every cell it covers must be free grass. A walk-through prop goes at a random point in its cell, which must be free and not next to a path. Every prop also keeps `spacing` from the other props of its layer.
7. **Mobs**: `mobCount` spawns of `mobPrefab` on cells inside the border band that the spawn can walk to (4-connected around the footprints, like the navigation grid's regions). Each one is away from props, at least `mobDistanceFromSpawn` from the spawn and `mobSpacing` from the other mobs. A warning reports any that did not fit.
8. **Campsite**, from the detail stream: a centre on interior grass deeper than the edge band plus `campsiteRadius`, with no path within that radius plus the path clearance, its radius clear of the spawn's disc and of every mob; among such cells, one with the fewest cells under blocking props within the radius less half a cell, where the fire and the seats go. The props in that circle are removed, a wobbly patch of dirt of the radius is laid around the centre (on grass off the paths), and the plants on or beside it go too, since a plant's sprite stands above its cell. `campfirePrefab` stands in the middle and `seatCount` of `campsiteSeats` on the dirt between 1.5 cells and the radius less half a cell from it, `seatSpacing` apart. A tree at the patch's rim stays and stands on the dirt. Props are only removed, so nothing else moves and the spawn reaches all it reached before. A warning reports a clearing with no such cell; none of 20 seeds has one.

All randomness comes from a `System.Random` seeded with the seed (the campsite from the second stream), and every loop walks ordered lists rather than hash sets, so a seed always gives the same layout. A generation takes about 17 ms.

The Clearing lists grass, dirt, gravel and stone. Its asset has four scatter layers: dense trees along the edge, a dozen trees inside, bushes, rocks and a stump, and walk-through plants; its campsite is the `Campfire` with three seats from `Stump_Small`, `Stool_A` and `Stool_B`, on dirt 3 cells in radius. Seed 1 places 144 props, the campsite at (1, 7). Until Phase 3 of TerrainTypes.md the Clearing also listed sand, mud and water, generated empty for a pond painted by hand in `Area_Clearing.unity`; the pond went when the area was regenerated, and with it the layers. `Plant_Fern` is not in the plant list: it was left out while its sprite still showed a sliver of the neighbouring tile, and that slice is now fixed. Adding it back changes what every seed generates, so regenerate the Clearing afterwards if you want it there.

### What the PlayMode tests rely on

The Clearing tests boot into the generated scene. `PlayerControlsPlayModeTests` builds a seven-cell wall two cells right of the spawn and walks around it, and also needs five clear cells in a straight line beside it: `spawnClearRadius` 6 gives that room. Mobs spawn at least 14 cells from the spawn, more than twice their detection radius, and the tests that watch one mob deactivate the others. The campsite keeps its radius outside the spawn's clear disc and away from the mobs, so it changes neither margin. Keep these margins when retuning the Clearing, or adjust the tests.

`AreaGenerationTests` checks over 20 seeds that the Clearing's paths lie on grass with the spawn on the road and its disc open, footprints on grass and apart, every mob reachable and spread out, and a campsite on a patch of dirt on the grass, off the paths, outside the spawn's disc and deeper than the edge band, with one campfire and every seat on the dirt and no plant on or beside it; and, for each generator, that a copy with its detail-stream step turned off lays every other layer, mob, the spawn and every prop the same (the campsite's props and those it replaced aside).

## The Village Forest generator

One larger area, ringed by forest, that joins three environments: a village of cottages along a stone street that enters from the map edge, an open clearing, and a dense forest crossed by winding gravel trails. One generation, in order:

1. **Grass**: as in the Clearing, from `size`, `squareness` and `edgeWobble`. The asset's 92 x 62 with squareness 4 is nearly rectangular.
2. **Regions**: the village is a blob of `villageSize` against the west or east edge (picked at random), moved inwards by up to three cells; the clearing is a blob of `clearingSize` on the other side of the middle, off to the north or south. Both are superellipses whose radius wobbles by `regionWobble`. Every grass cell in the band `borderDepth` deep along the edge, and every cell outside both blobs, is forest, so the forest rings the whole area and fills the rest.
3. **Street**: stone, `streetWidth` wide: a spline through four points from beyond the village's edge of the map, through the village's middle row, to the village's inner end, the junction. Each point is within `streetMeander` cells of that row. Keep the meander small: the cottages line up along the street.
4. **Spawn**: on the street at `spawnAlongStreet` (0 at the map edge, 1 at the junction), or the nearest point along it with grass all around for `spawnClearRadius` cells. The disc around it is kept free of blocking props from here on.
5. **Square**: a disc of plaza of `squareRadius` opening south off the street at `squareAlongStreet`, around a bed of grass that holds the fountain's footprint with `pathClearance` cells around it, so the fountain keeps its clearance from the paving like any blocking prop. The disc touches the street, which is drawn over the plaza where they meet; any street cell inside it is paved too, under the stone. The plaza counts as a path for every later step, as stone did.
6. **Buildings**: walking the street from the map edge towards the junction, a lot is tried at every column, and after one fits the next starts `lotGap` columns further on. A lot is the building's width plus `sideYard` columns on each side; from the street up it holds a row of clearance, the fence row, `yardDepth` rows of yard, the building, then `backYard` rows. The Cottage's sprite shows its front wall and porch, so its front is its south side and it faces the street. Every column of a lot must border the street, the lot must be grass off the paths, the edge band and the clearing, the building must stay out of the spawn's disc, and the lot must end `LotMargin` columns (half the trail width plus the clearance plus one) before the junction, where the trails start. A one-cell stone path runs from the street up to the doorstep, the cell in front of the door: `doorColumn` counts from the footprint's left, and 3 puts the Cottage's doorstep under its porch. The doorstep touches the building, so it is the one path cell allowed within a building's clearance. Every cell of a built lot joins the village, even where the village's wobbly outline fell short of it. A warning reports fewer than `buildingCount` buildings.
7. **Trails**: gravel, `trailWidth` wide. The first runs from the junction to the clearing's middle and on across it, away from the village. Then come `forestTrailCount` forest trails. Each aims at a forest cell at least `borderDepth + gladeRadius + 1` from the edge, picked at random among the eighth of them furthest from every path laid so far, and starts from the nearest point of the trails already laid. A trail bends about every nine cells, each bend pushed sideways by up to `trailWinding` of the distance between bends, unless that would take it into the edge band. A trail that would enter the village, come within `LotMargin` of a lot or leave the grass is dropped, the cells around its target are ruled out, and another target is tried, up to 24 times. Each forest trail ends in a glade, a disc of `gladeRadius` kept free of blocking props. Every trail starts on a path, so all the paths form one network. A path cell that touches that network only at a corner, left where the edge of the grass clipped a path, is then dropped.
8. **Open ground**: blocking props are kept off every path and the `pathClearance` cells beside it, and off the glades.
9. **Village furniture**: the fountain in the middle of the square; lamp posts about every `lampSpacing` cells along the street through the village, alternately north and south of it just beyond its clearance and outside the lots, so the northern ones stand in the gaps between yards rather than in their fences; a fence along each lot's front row, laid from either side of the gate outwards; `townPropsPerBuilding` town props in each yard, in front of and beside its building, and `townPropsAroundSquare` in a ring south of the square, each set `townPropSpacing` apart. The lots are then reserved, so the scatter layers put no blocking prop in a yard.
10. **Scatter layers**, in list order, each over the grass cells of its region, placed as in the Clearing.
11. **Mobs**: each region's count of `mobPrefab` (the clearing's, then the forest's, then the village's) on cells of that region the spawn can walk to, deeper than the edge band, not next to a blocking prop, at least `mobDistanceFromSpawn` from the spawn and `mobSpacing` from every other mob. A warning reports a region where fewer fit.
12. **Reachability**: once every prop stands, a warning names any path cell, doorstep or region the spawn cannot walk to (4-connected around the footprints, like the navigation grid's regions). The dense forest can seal off a few stray cells between trees, which matters to nothing; the paths, doors, mobs and regions are what is checked.
13. **Dirt**, from the detail stream: shoulders beside the gravel trails near the village. Each trail cell within `dirtReach` of the junction gives a width, `dirtWidth` at the junction falling to nothing at the reach; a grass cell off the paths and the lots is dirt when it lies within a trail cell's width, moved in and out by up to `dirtWobble` cells by three smooth waves 5 to 9 cells long at random angles. Dirt may run under a tree beside a trail; props stand on it as on grass.

A generation takes about 140 ms in the editor, eight times the Clearing's: the area is four times larger and holds over 500 blocking props.

The asset ([Generator_VillageForest.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_VillageForest.asset), seed 1 in `Area_VillageForest.unity`) builds three Cottages with `Fence_Iron`, `LampPost_Vine` and the `Fountain`, and furnishes yards and square from barrels, pots, an urn, a table, stools, a bench and a cauldron. Its scatter layers are village trees, bushes and flowers; clearing trees, bushes and rocks, and plants; then forest undergrowth (bushes, dark rocks, a large stump), forest trees and forest-floor plants. The forest trees layer asks for 2000 at a spacing of 2.2, more than ever fit, so it fills every spot it can. A prefab listed twice in a layer is picked twice as often: the forest trees list the oak, the round tree and the tall pine twice, so the twisted trees, which read as dead wood from a distance, stay a minority. Mobs: three in the clearing, six in the forest, none in the village. Seed 1 places 808 props (568 blocking), which makes the scene file about 5 MB; its square is 72 cells of plaza and its trails 37 cells of dirt (`dirtWidth` 2, `dirtReach` 18, `dirtWobble` 0.75).

Like the Clearing, it keeps `spawnClearRadius` 6 and mobs at least 14 cells from the spawn, so it would satisfy the PlayMode tests' margins if it became the starting area.

`AreaGenerationTests` checks over 20 seeds that there are no warnings; that every region has at least 300 cells, the street crosses the village and trails reach the clearing and the forest; that the paths lie on grass, free of footprints, as one 4-connected network through the spawn; that the forest is more than three times as dense with footprints as the clearing; that at least 99% of the open ground of the village and the clearing, and 97% of the forest's, can be walked to from the spawn; that every configured building stands in the village with a stone doorstep the spawn can reach and no other path within its clearance; that there is one fountain in a plaza square, at least two lamp posts and a fence per building; that the square is plaza on grass within its radius of the fountain and opens onto the street; that the dirt lies on grass off the paths, under no building or fence, within its width of a trail and its reach of the street; that the spawn is on the street with open grass around it; that footprints are on grass, apart, and (buildings aside) clear of the paths' clearance; and that every region has its configured mobs, most of them in the forest, all reachable, far enough from the spawn and from each other.

## The Lakeside generator

A lakeside that shows most terrain types in one area: grass ringed by trees, a lake in the north, a sandy beach on its southern shore and a muddy marsh around the rest of it, a stone road crossing the south, a gravel trail from the road down to the beach, patches of mud and dry grass and small shallow ponds on the open grass. One generation, in order:

1. **Grass**: as in the Clearing, from `size`, `squareness` and `edgeWobble`. The asset's 64 x 46 is a third larger than the Clearing.
2. **Stone road**: as the Clearing's, through five points from beyond the west edge to beyond the east edge, but around `roadSouth` of the half height south of the middle, wandering by `roadMeander`.
3. **Lake**: a wobbly blob (squareness 2.5) of `lakeSize`, centred `lakeNorth` of the half height north of the middle and up to a fifth of the half width off it to either side, filled with water on the grass cells deeper than the edge band plus two and at least `roadMargin` from the road (8 neighbours); only its largest 4-connected region is kept. A lake under `minLakeCells` gives a warning. Water is painted over the grass, which goes on under it like any lower layer.
4. **Shore**: every dry cell records its distance in steps from the water. Cells facing south from the lake's centre, within `beachSpread` degrees of straight south, are beach (sand) up to `beachWidth` from the water; the rest of the shore is marsh (mud) up to `marshWidth`. Both widths swell and thin around the lake by up to `shoreWobble` of themselves, with two random harmonics of the angle, and are at least a cell. Path cells are left as they are.
5. **Trail**: gravel, `trailWidth` wide, from the road point straight below to the beach cell nearest the lake's south point that does not touch the water, bending once on the way. It is drawn over the beach where they meet.
6. **Mud**: `marshPatchCount` wobbly patches, radius between the two values of `mudPatchRadius`, centred on random marsh cells, so the marsh spreads beyond the shore in places; then `mudPatchCount` more on random interior cells away from the lake, its shore and the paths. Mud never covers the water, the beach, a path or the edge band.
7. **Spawn**: on the road at `spawnAlongRoad`, as in the Clearing, with its disc of `spawnClearRadius` kept free of blocking props.
8. **Open ground**: blocking props are kept off the paths and the `pathClearance` cells beside them, the spawn's disc, the water, the beach and the mud.
9. **Scatter layers**, in list order, over the dry grass that is neither beach nor mud and does not touch the water. `Border` is the edge band `borderDepth` deep, `Shore` the grass within the beach's or the marsh's width plus `shoreZoneDepth` of the water, `Interior` the rest.
10. **Mobs**: `mobCount` spawns on dry cells inside the border band that the spawn can walk to around the footprints and the water, away from props, at least `mobDistanceFromSpawn` from the spawn and `mobSpacing` apart. A warning reports any that did not fit.
11. **Reachability**: a warning counts any beach or trail cell the spawn cannot walk to.
12. **Dry grass**, from the detail stream: `dryGrassPatchCount` wobbly patches, radius between the two values of `dryGrassPatchRadius`, centred on random open grass cells inside the edge band, beyond the shore band and off the paths and the mud. Dry grass never covers water or beach, keeps a cell of grass inside the map's edge, and drops any piece of fewer than four cells left where a patch was cut short. It keeps a cell of grass between itself and the mud and the paths: those are drawn over it, so the renderer takes it to go on under them, and beside one with only grass under it the straw would show under its rounded corners where the two meet grass (see TerrainTypes.md's Phase 3 notes). Props stand on it as on grass.
13. **Shallow ponds**, from the detail stream after the dry grass: `shallowPondCount` wobbly patches of shallows, radius between the two values of `shallowPondRadius`, centred on random cells where a pond fits. A pond cell and its 8 neighbours are plain grass (no dry grass, beach, mud, path or water, two cells inside the map's edge) with no blocking prop or mob on them, outside the spawn's clear disc, and the ponds keep two cells apart, so a pond meets only grass and no third look shows under its corners; pieces of fewer than four cells are dropped. The plants (walk-through props) on or beside a pond or up to three rows below it are removed, since a plant's sprite stands above its cell. Units wade the ponds at the shallows' cost. The lake stays all deep water: shallows beside it, as a ring along the beach and a ford, were tried in Phase 4 of TerrainTypes.md and did not look good with the pond water.

A generation takes about 50 ms. The asset ([Generator_Lakeside.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_Lakeside.asset), seed 1 in `Area_Lakeside.unity`) uses the Clearing's prefabs: 280 trees along the edge at a spacing of 2, 16 trees inside, bushes and rocks and plants both near the shore and inside, and four Weasels, and five dry grass patches of 2.5 to 5 cells; three shallow ponds of 2 to 3 cells. Seed 1 places 237 props, 104 cells of dry grass and three ponds of 10, 17 and 13 cells (8 plants removed for them).

`AreaGenerationTests` checks over 20 seeds that there are no warnings; that every terrain layer has cells and lies on the grass; that dry grass lies only on open grass, not on or beside mud, a path or the map's edge, in pieces of four cells or more; that the lake covers no path, beach, mud or pond and keeps `roadMargin` from the road; that the beach lies along the water and the trail reaches it; that the spawn is on the road with no footprint or water within its clear radius; that footprints cover dry grass that is neither beach nor mud, apart and clear of the paths' clearance, and walk-through props stand on such grass off the paths and away from the water; and that the spawn can walk to the whole beach and to every mob, placed apart and far enough from it. It also checks that the lake is the same with or without the ponds; that there are one to `shallowPondCount` ponds of four cells or more, two cells apart, each cell and its neighbours plain grass with no prop or mob, outside the spawn's disc, and no third look under a rounded corner around them; that turning the ponds off leaves every other layer, the spawn and every mob as they were and every prop but the plants they removed; and, on a written layout, that the ponds are waded at the shallows' cost of 20 and the water is not.

## Terrain layers and drawing order

Every area's ground layers follow one project-wide draw order, the terrain style's layer order (`TerrainStyle.LayerOrder`, see [TerrainTypes.md](TerrainTypes.md#decisions)): grass, dry grass, dirt, sand, mud, gravel, plaza, stone, shallows, water today, with the planned materials to go in at their places. Each generator lists only the layers its areas use, as a subsequence of that order, so an area pays texture memory only for its own layers, and a pair of materials is drawn the same way up in every area.

| Layer | Tilemap | Tile | Material | Terrain type | Listed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grass | `GrassTilemap` | `Tile_Grass` | `Grass` | `Terrain_Grass` | every generator |
| Dry grass | `DryGrassTilemap` | `Tile_DryGrass` | `DryGrass` | `Terrain_DryGrass` | the Lakeside |
| Dirt | `DirtTilemap` | `Tile_Dirt` | `Dirt` | `Terrain_Dirt` | the Clearing (its campsite), the Village Forest (along its trails) |
| Sand | `SandTilemap` | `Tile_Sand` | `Sand` | `Terrain_Sand` | the Lakeside |
| Mud | `MudTilemap` | `Tile_Mud` | `Mud` | `Terrain_Mud` | the Lakeside |
| Gravel | `GravelTilemap` | `Tile_Gravel` | `Gravel` | `Terrain_Gravel` | every generator |
| Plaza | `PlazaTilemap` | `Tile_Plaza` | `Plaza` | `Terrain_Plaza` | the Village Forest (its square) |
| Stone | `StoneTilemap` | `Tile_Stone` | `Stone` | `Terrain_Stone` | every generator |
| Shallows | `ShallowsTilemap` | `Tile_Shallows` | `Shallows` | `Terrain_Shallows` | the Lakeside |
| Water | `WaterTilemap` | `Tile_Water` | `Water` | `Terrain_Water` | the Lakeside |
| Walls | `CollisionTilemap` (not generated) | `walls_DualGridRuleTile`'s data tile | none (its render tilemap draws them) | blocking | order 10 on the `Terrain` sorting layer |

So the Clearing lists grass, dirt, gravel and stone, the Village Forest grass, dirt, gravel, plaza and stone, and the Lakeside grass, dry grass, sand, mud, gravel, stone, shallows and water, each with `TerrainStyle.asset` as its terrain style. A layer's index is its place in its area's list, so it differs between areas: `AreaLayoutBuilder` and the generators reach a layer through the generator's own layer field, never a fixed index. Tiles are in `Assets/Tiles/Ground`, materials in `Assets/Settings/Terrain/Materials`. The area's `TerrainBlendRenderer` draws the layers in one shader on the `Terrain` sorting layer at order 0, bottom first, so stone is drawn over gravel, the paths over mud and sand, and water over everything where they meet, and a layer's place is also its navigation priority, so the layer drawn on top of a cell decides it. Grass is painted under the paths too: lower layers continue under upper ones, and grass over nothing is the map's edge. Walls sit at order 10 in every area, so they draw over the ground. To paint a material an area has no layer for, add the layer to the area's generator and run Convert Ground; the Terrain Brush says so in the Tile Palette when its picked tile is such a material's.

`TerrainMovement_Default` has no rules for dry grass, dirt, sand, gravel, plaza or stone, so they are walkable at the default cost of 10, the same as grass. Making roads cheaper would make units prefer them, but it would also lower the profile's minimum cost, which the A* heuristic uses as its step cost, and it would stop click-to-move from taking its straight-line shortcut across grass. Clicks would then bend toward the road. Mud and the shallows cost 20, which leaves the minimum at 10: paths go around mud when the detour costs less, and the shortcut is not taken across it. Water is not walkable, and since it is drawn on top it decides its cells whatever lies under it, so paths go around a pond and a click on water walks to the nearest walkable cell. The Lakeside's shallow ponds are walked at 20. The terrain types are there for later uses such as footsteps or speed.

## Multiplayer

Generation happens in the editor, and its result is saved in the scene. Every machine loads the same terrain and props, and nothing is generated or replicated at runtime. Mobs are only spawn markers. The host spawns them as network objects through `AreaMobSpawner`, as in any area.

## Adding a generator

1. Subclass `AreaGenerator` with `[CreateAssetMenu(menuName = "TopDownRPG/World/...")]`. List the terrain layers it paints in `TerrainLayers` (only the ones its areas use, bottom first, each with its tilemap name, plain tile, terrain type and material; at most 16, their materials in the order of the style's layer order) and fill one `AreaLayout` of those layers per call to `Generate(seed)`, reaching each layer through its own field. Give its asset a terrain style. Build it on an `AreaLayoutBuilder` for the grass, painting, reservations, scatter, mobs and reachability, and derive its scatter layers from `PropScatter`.
2. Keep it deterministic: one `System.Random` from the seed (the builder's), and no iteration over hash sets where order matters. Terrain added to an existing generator after its props and mobs goes on the builder's detail stream (`StartDetailStream`), so existing layouts keep their props and mobs.
3. Keep paths on terrain, the spawn open, footprints apart and every mob reachable from the spawn, and test those properties over many seeds as `AreaGenerationTests` does for the three generators. Add its asset path to the determinism, writer, layer list and template test cases.
4. Create its asset under `Assets/Settings/Generation`. The window lists every `AreaGenerator` asset.
