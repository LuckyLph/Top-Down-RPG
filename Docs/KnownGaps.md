# Known gaps

Plan for closing the known gaps that no other plan covers (Architecture.md's Known gaps and oddities): each one fixed, or recorded as accepted with its reason. It turns the Known gaps feature from [Roadmap.md](Roadmap.md)'s Later into phases. Tick off the phase checklist as steps land; a gap fixed comes off Architecture.md's list in the same commit, and a gap accepted stays on it, marked accepted, with the reason and a link here.

Every rule in [Multiplayer.md](Multiplayer.md#rules-for-new-code-starting-now) applies. Multiplayer is checked by the PlayMode tests that run in the editor (an in-process host and client); checking by hand with Multiplayer Play Mode or a second machine is out of scope here and comes with a later feature of its own ([Roadmap.md](Roadmap.md)).

## Where we start

Architecture.md lists about 60 known gaps. Six are covered elsewhere or settled and stay out of this plan: the fixed build version (multiplayer testing, in Roadmap.md's Later), lava only blocking (lava damage, in Later), the placeholder status icons (DesignSystem.md's Later, by the user's choice), the camera off the art grid (LightingGaps.md's Phase 7), the editor's night as a preview (LightingGaps.md's Phase 6) and every player visible to every client (fine for 2 to 4 players). The rest, by area, with what this plan proposes for each in the triage below:

- **Gameplay**: waystones and world exits with no art; a trip's arrival unprotected; a bow shooting into a wall in range; unit picks ignoring the ability's filter; the weapon switch not saved; nothing physical at a world's edge, water, the ocean, lava or the cliff of raised ground; the walk and sprint never shown; feet sliding going north or south.
- **Multiplayer**: one save per install, a client keeping no copy of its place; remote arrows late; a copy's cast and windup on its own clock; a client's attacks seldom holding their contact frame; hit directions each machine's own; chunk holds outliving trips.
- **Worlds and streaming**: chunk load hitches and per-renderer texture arrays in the editor; a generated chunk keeping layers it no longer needs; a moved place taking the nearest walkable cell, reachable or not; coast and glow maps baked only on request; persistent ids repaired only for undoable edits; world search limits as code defaults; saved terrain edits naming layers by index; the Overworld's wave crests on end at its top edge and the sand nubs at the Coast region's joins.
- **Terrain and the Terrain Brush**: one layer with waves per area and the coast map's rebuild while painting; the walls' gap at the ground's corners; diagonal paths stepping; planks picking a way per cell; a layer showing a lower one under its corners; a bridge's end depending on the layers between; no preview for a flood fill, on raised ground or for walls across a chunk border; one level of raised ground; ground tiles found by name; the layer order unchecked; a scratch scene's grass layer; the soft look's first frames in the editor.
- **Lighting**: props darkening whole under the sun; the walls' light shadows by screen pixel; soft light shadows off the art grid; restoring our shadow shader rewriting URP's settings; Shadow Caster 2Ds making meshes in the editor, and the first dusk's frame.
- **Animation and the editor**: a buffered cast replacing the release; a cast aimed aside running toward its aim; the recipe inspector's cast over the run as one strip; TMP's fallback font changing in the editor.
- **Code against CLAUDE.md**: inline comments; `MobConfig`'s hard-coded layer mask and create-menu paths outside `TopDownRPG/`; static mutable state (`SceneQuery`, `UnityClock.Shared`); two classes in `SceneDefinitionEditor.cs`; the status dev panel finding its scope itself; duplicated tuning; unused or test-only API, the old sword icon and the dual-grid leftovers.

## Goals

- Every gap no plan covers either fixed or accepted with a reason, so Architecture.md's list holds only what is known and chosen.
- What a player meets first fixed first: the places they look for, the orders that misbehave, what they lose between sessions.
- The code back within CLAUDE.md's rules.
- Nothing a phase fixes changing what the other machines see without a PlayMode test of the host and client.

## Non-goals (for now)

- New features a gap points to but does not need: a second level of raised ground, split animation layers, knockbacks and dashes and the colliders they would need, a slot list for the saves in a menu.
- The status icons, which wait for DesignSystem.md, and adopting that proposal.
- The six gaps covered elsewhere (Where we start).

#### Phase 1 notes

- Inline comments: `IClock.cs`, `SceneDefinition.cs`, `SceneBuildValidator` and `SceneDefinitionEditor` had none left. The rationale in `PlayModeBootstrapper`'s and `VContainerDiagnosticsBuildGuard`'s went to Architecture.md (Boot sequence, Editor tools); the test files' went into their tests' summaries and assertion messages. Shader and HLSL files keep their comments: HLSL has no XML docs, so those comments are its functions' descriptors.
- `MobConfig`'s obstacle mask: both assets (`Mob_Default`, `Mob_StressTest`) already held the `Obstacles` layer, so dropping the code default changed neither; a test checks every asset keeps it. An empty mask logs an error from the mob's perception and, as before, sees through everything. `MobConfig.Create` defaults the mask to the `Obstacles` layer by name, as the assets set it. The menus: `TopDownRPG/AI/Mob Config`, `TopDownRPG/AI/Navigation/Terrain Type`, `TopDownRPG/AI/Navigation/Terrain Movement Profile`, `TopDownRPG/World/Dual Grid Rule Tile`.
- Static state: `SceneQuery` reads a scene's roots into an array per call (its callers run on loads and builds, never per frame). `UnityClock.Shared` is gone: the Main scope registers a `UnityClock` singleton, and the five components that fell back to it (`PlayerAbilities`, `CharacterVisuals`, `MeleeDamageDealer`, `PlayerController`, `PlayerWeaponController`) log an error from `Start` without an injected clock. Every scope that builds them (Gameplay, world and chunk scopes, the in-process client) already resolved the Main scope's clock, so nothing changes in play; `PlayerAbilities` draws no cast gizmos in edit mode, where it has no clock.
- The status dev panel: accepted, the lookup kept, for it and the other three panels that share it (Architecture.md's Known gaps has the reason). There is no DevTools scope to own a registration: the panels live outside every scope, `LifetimeScope.Enqueue` is a global stack that pops whatever was pushed last (a panel's lifelong installer would pop the streaming benchmark runner's or a test's instead), and scene objects would be missing scripts in release builds.
- Duplicated tuning: the navigation keeps the nearest-cell radius (a standalone `NavigationGrid2D`'s field, a world's map's default, 8 each) and `MobConfig` drops its own (8 on both assets), so a mob searches as far as A* does. `MobMotor2D`'s speed and acceleration and `MeleeDamageDealer`'s damage and interval are plain fields with their old defaults, set from the config in `Initialize`, which the brain always calls with one (it disables itself without). The Weasel prefab and the two mob configs were saved again through the editor to drop the old keys; the prefab's save also wrote fields added since it was last saved at their defaults (the visuals' cast light and lantern, the hit feedback's lantern, all empty, and the cast light's intensity of 1).
- Unused and test-only API. Removed, nothing using them: `TerrainType2D.TerrainId` with its `terrainId` field (the asset is the tag; the eighteen terrain assets saved again without it), `ScreenFader.IsOpaque`, `PlayerWeaponController.SetAttackSpeed` (no test used it either; attack speed stays 1 on the wire until statuses bring their own setter), `PlayerClass.DisplayName` with its field (the placeholder class saved again), and `MobController.onAttackRangeEntered` with `InvokeAttackRangeEntered` (no listener anywhere; the Weasel saved again). Made `internal`, for tests (Gameplay and Core grant both test assemblies their internals, Gameplay the editor too): `PlayerWeaponController.CurrentWeaponName` and `EquipFromCatalog`, `ProjectileService.UnitStruck`, `AbilityService.CastApplied` and `CastResolved`, `AreaTerrain.TrySetCells` (also the editor's dev menu), `SessionSave.PlayerCount` and `WorldCount`, `WorldState.ChunkCount`, `ChunkState.DeadMobCount`. `StaticSlash.png` is gone: the HUD prefab's weapon icon, which pointed at it until the presenter sets the weapon's icon (or none) as it starts, points at no sprite, and `PlayerWeaponSystemTests` builds its icon in code. `DualGridRuleTile.DataTile` throws one `InvalidOperationException` naming the tile and how to fix it, with no separate error logged.
- Tests: the touched classes' tests pass, in EditMode and PlayMode. Two changed their setup only: `CombatComponentTests`' dealer without an `AttackService` is now given a clock, which the fallback used to supply, and `PlayerWeaponSystemTests` builds its icon. New: every `MobConfig` asset's obstacle mask and the perception's error without one (`MobStateMachineTests`), and `DataTile`'s error (`DualGridTilemapModuleTests`). Phase 1 changes nothing a player or another machine sees.

#### Phase 2 notes

- Unit picks: `PointerTargetPicker.Pick(point, filter)` overlaps only the layers of the teams an `AbilityUnitFilter` accepts (either team by default). The command carries two picks: `Target`, either team, for a move press or hold, and `AbilityTarget`, picked with the unit filter of the local player's ability in the pressed slot (`LocalPlayer.Abilities`, through `LocalPlayerTracker`), so a move press on the same frame keeps its own pick. An ability not aimed at a unit, an empty slot or no local player picks either team, as before. The hover ring still shows enemies only.
- Line of sight: `PlayerControlSettings.ObstacleLayers` (the `Obstacles` layer, as `MobConfig`'s; a test checks every asset) and `IUnitQueries.InSight(target, radius)`, a cast of the attack's sweep radius (the projectile's for a ranged weapon, 0 for a melee one, `PlayerOrderContext.AttackSightRadius`) from the player's feet toward the middle of the target's collider, as an arrow flies, up to where it first touches the target. So a wall corner an arrow would clip counts as a wall, while one behind the target does not. Every weapon and the attack move go by it: in range but out of sight, the order keeps chasing (paths go around walls) until the target is in sight, then stops and swings. It costs one cast a tick while the target is in range. A target behind a wall no path gets round keeps the order chasing to the nearest reachable cell, as a target out of reach did before; walls and distance still never cancel an attack. Casts still ignore walls: `AbilityService` resolves through them, which no gap lists.
- Trip protection: `TripProtection`, where game state is decided, protects a player from the trip's approval (`LocalTripAuthority.Send`, so the fade out too) until its machine reports fading in at the end of that trip (`ITripAuthority.ReportArrived`: `LocalTripTraveller` after `GameFlow.TravelAsync`; a client's report goes to the host as `PlayerWorldSync.TripArrivedRpc` naming the trip, and an earlier trip's report ends nothing), or `GameplaySettings.TripProtectionSeconds` (10 s of game time) pass, with a warning. Untargetable means out of the world's `WorldPlayers`, so its mobs drop the traveller and do not take it, and `HitService` ignores hits on it, damage and statuses alike (a buff from an ally too; a heal still lands). `StatusEffectService` holds its statuses still, as it held a client's player only until that client reported a chunk; the host now rewrites a unit's replicated statuses as a hold ends, since the clients counted them down through it. A dead player's respawn trip also waits in `WorldStreaming.ArriveAsync` for the player to be alive before its fade in (at most the same 10 s), so a client no longer shows itself dead for a round trip after fading in.
- The saved weapon: `GameSaveData` version 3, each player's record naming the catalog weapon it holds by its id, the weapon asset's GUID (`PlayerWeapon.WeaponId`, kept by the editor as a world's id is; `WeaponCatalog.IdOf`, `FindById`), as Multiplayer.md's rules ask of assets in saves, so a reordered catalog or a renamed asset keeps the saves, and only a removed weapon drops from them (a warning, and the starting weapon). A version 2 save still reads, its players with no weapon, and is written over as version 3; version 1 and newer formats are ignored and kept as before. `GameSaveRecorder` records the weapon with the place on every save and as a player leaves, as the host sees it (a client's copy follows its owner's switches). `GameplayPlayers` hands `PlayerSpawner` the `SessionSave`'s weapon of each player id: offline and the host's own player equip it before spawning; a client's player gets it through a server-written network variable on `PlayerNetworkSync` (`SetSavedWeapon` before the spawn), which the owner equips and every copy shows until the owner writes its own, so the host's first save after a join already holds it. The protocol gained that variable and the arrival message; the build version stays pre-alpha (CLAUDE.md).
- Throwaway sessions (added with the user): pressing Play with a world's scene (or the Gameplay scene) open starts a new game there as before, but `BootFlow` starts it as a throwaway (`SessionSave.StartThrowaway`, logged once), and `GameSaveRecorder` writes nothing while `SessionSave.IsThrowaway`: entering the world, the autosave, a trip, leaving to the menu and quitting all leave the save file as the last game started from the main menu left it. The session keeps its state in memory as any does (dead mobs, terrain edits, discoveries), so play inside it is unchanged. New Game, Continue and Host Game from the menu start real sessions again, in the same Play session too. `WorldPersistencePlayModeTests`' save-and-continue test now continues a save from the menu instead of booting into the Sandbox.
- Accepted: a world's edge and water, the ocean, lava and the cliff without colliders, the walk and the sprint, and the feet going north or south, each marked in Architecture.md's list with its reason.
- Tests: EditMode `PlayerTargetingTests` (the filtered picks, the ability press's pick, `InSight`, the settings asset), `PlayerOrdersTests` (an enemy in range behind a wall chased until in sight), `WorldTripTests` (the protection from approval to the trip's own report, the time limit, a player leaving, the report through the link), `WorldPlayersTests` (a protected traveller left out and dropped by a mob), `StatusEffectTests` (no hit lands out of play and statuses stand still), `GameSaveTests` (the weapon round trip, a version 2 save read and rewritten, a throwaway session marked until a real one starts), `PlayerWeaponSystemTests` (every catalog weapon's id its asset's GUID, found by it). PlayMode `PlayerControlsPlayModeTests` (a longbow at a mob behind a wall walks round it before its first arrow, which hits), `WaystonePlayModeTests` (the host's own traveller and a client's untouched by a mob by the entry until they fade in, then hurt, on both machines for the client), `GameSavePlayModeTests` (the weapon restored on Continue, on Host Game and for a known client on both machines, and a switch saved as the host leaves; an editor boot into Testlands writing nothing over a stored save, which Continue then loads and saves again). The tests that build the in-process containers, the status and hit services and the trip authorities give them the protection.

#### Phase 4 notes

- The delayed connection (`NetworkDelay`, in the PlayMode tests): the Network Simulator writes its preset to every Unity Transport driver in the process, and a driver's simulator stage delays and drops what it sends and what it receives alike, so in one process each way went through it twice. Measured with the preset `NetworkSmoothnessPlayModeTests` uses (150 ms, 20 ms jitter, 4% loss): a 609 ms round trip, 309 ms out and 300 back, 9 of 80 unreliable pings lost (`NetworkDelayPlayModeTests`' explicit benchmark), so that test's runs and Architecture.md's Transform smoothing table were measured at about 300 ms each way. `NetworkDelay.Install()`, before a session starts, builds every driver through a driver constructor of its own (`UnityTransport.s_DriverConstructor`, Netcode's hook for it) with its simulator stage on sent packets only and a fixed seed (`NetworkDelay.Seed` and a count), so loss and jitter fall on the same packets run after run; it turns drivers already running the same way when it sets conditions, and disables the Network Simulators while installed, since each writes its preset over every driver as one is created. `Set(conditions)` gives every driver, and every one created later, half the round trip, give or take the jitter, and the loss, both ways alike; `SetSending(manager, delay, jitter, loss)` one manager's way out (100 drops all it sends); `Dispose` puts drivers back. Settings (`NetworkDelay.Conditions`): Fast (50 ms round trip, 5 ms jitter each way, 1% loss each way), Typical (150, 15, 2%), Slow (300, 30, 4%). Measured with 80 pings a setting on the clock both share, at 3.8 ms a frame: no delay 7 ms (4 out, 4 back, the frames a message waits); Fast 55 ms (30, 26), none lost; Typical 158 ms (80, 77), 4 of 80 unreliable pings lost; Slow 301 ms (154, 149), 5 lost. The reliable channel loses nothing, and its quickest pings take the setting's round trip; as it delivers in order, a lost packet holds the ones after it until its resend: its median rose to 171 ms at Typical and 596 ms at Slow (p90 897).
- The measurements (`NetworkTimingPlayModeTests`, explicit benchmarks, 30 attacks, 14 casts and 20 shots a setting, run before the changes): a copy's held windup past its own (the contact message arriving after the start message plus the windup) had a median of 2, 9, 9 and 14 ms at no delay, Fast, Typical and Slow, up to 17, 209 and 351 ms, 1 of 30 over 200 ms at Typical and at Slow, where a lost packet's resend held it. A copy's cast on its own clock released, against the host resolving it on the completion message, within -4 to +17 ms at Fast, -15 to +27 ms at Typical and -16 to +36 ms at Slow for 13 of 14 casts, the 14th 446 ms early, its completion lost and resent; a cancel arriving in such a window followed a release that never happened. A client's own sword hits on a Weasel standing in reach held its contact frame 30 of 30 times at no delay, 1 at Fast and none at Typical and Slow, its hit showing a round trip after its contact (median 56, 158 and 305 ms); its own view at contact (its copy of the Weasel in the weapon's area) agreed with the host's hit 30 of 30 times at every setting, and with the Weasel sliding across the reach at its run speed (a stress case: mobs stand to attack) 27, 24, 24 and 16 of 30, wrong both ways about equally. A remote arrow (the host's, on the client, its projectiles flown each frame) left one way late (median 27, 72 and 149 ms), struck the copy every time standing or sliding (it homes), and its hit reached the client a median 4 to 8 ms after it landed at every setting (p10 to p90 about ±30 ms at Slow); flown ahead by its message's age it would land about one way before its hit shows.
- Hit origins (widened with the user from arrows to every hit): a hit carries the point it came from as the machine that resolved it saw it: `Health.ApplyDamage`, `Health.DamageEvent.Origin`, `DamageService.ApplyDamage` and `ApplyReplicatedHit`, `DamageReport.Origin` and `HitService.ApplyHit` take it (null for none: a tick, an immune hit, a hit applied directly). An arrow's is the point it was shot from (`ProjectileFlight.Origin`, its shooter's feet at the shot, on the host the owner's position from its contact message); a melee hit's where the attacker stood (`AttackService.Resolve`, the contact's position for a player, the mob's own for a mob); a cast's where the caster stood as it completed (`AbilityService.Resolve`'s caster position, the owner's from its completion message). `NetworkHealth.HitRpc` carries it (`origin`, `hasOrigin`), and `HitFeedbackPresenter` turns the nudge, the impact's side and the camera kick from it, falling back to where this machine shows the source for a hit without one. A shooter that moves after its shot, a caster running through Whirl or Smite and a lagging copy no longer turn a hit differently on different machines. The protocol gained the origin; the build version stays pre-alpha.
- The copy's windup: an action played until its contact holds its last windup frame at most a contact wait past its windup (`CharacterLocomotionSettings.ContactWaitSeconds`, 0.25 s on both characters' settings, a field of the animator's settings asset since the animator reads it), then ends, the character going back to its locomotion; a contact arriving later shows nothing, while its hit still lands (`ResolveRemoteContact`, apart from the visuals). 0.25 s covers every contact measured at Fast and Typical but one at 209 ms, and all but one at Slow (351 ms), which then shows no swing rather than a quarter-second freeze; the same wait bounds a copy's cast waiting for its completion and the owner's own cast, which its orders always complete on the update the cast time runs out. Mobs' copies, which play the host's attacks the same way, get it too.
- The chunk holds: `ClientChunkReadiness` records the trip each held chunk was last reported in. `BeginTrip(client, trip, world)` (`IClientReadiness.BeginTrip` now names the trip's world, from `PlayerWorldSync`) releases the client's chunks of every other world at once, hiding their objects from it, so the host may unload them; those of the trip's world stay held (`Holds`), their objects still shown, but count as ready for the trip (`IsReady`, `IClientReadiness.IsClientReady(client, chunk)`) only once reported during it, which the client's streaming does for every loaded chunk of its world as the trip reaches it. A respawn, a trip within the player's world, so waits for its waystone's chunk reported in that trip, as the host's own respawn waits for its own streaming to have the chunk loaded. Visibility goes by holds (`NetworkSession.Spawn(instance, homeChunk)`), so a respawn hides nothing.
- The copy's cast release (decided): the owner's completion (`PlayerNetworkSync.CastCompletedRpc`) now goes to every other machine (`SendTo.NotOwner`, owner-invoked), the host's own casts' too, where it went to the host alone for a client's casts; each copy shows the release on the update it arrives (`PlayerAbilities.CompleteRemoteCast`, `RemoteCastCompleted`, which `PlayerCastVisuals` turns into the release, its gather played until release as the owner's is), and the host then resolves a client's cast, so the release shows on the update the cast lands there, and on another client's machine with the hits the host sends as it resolves. A cancel arriving after the cast time stops a gather still waiting, so no false release shows. The protocol changed the completion's target; the build version stays pre-alpha.
- The client's contact hold (decided): at its own melee contact a client predicts its hit (`AttackService.Predict`, off the authority only, for a weapon that fires no projectile): a living unit in the weapon's area that is not the attacker, not of its faction and not immune to the weapon's damage type raises `CombatEvents.HitPredicted`, which `HitFeedbackPresenter` turns into the attacker's `HoldContact`, as a landed hit's.
