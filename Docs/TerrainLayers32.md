# 32 terrain layers

Experiment: raise the number of ground layers one terrain renderer draws from 16 to 32, so the area template can hold every material of the style's order (eighteen today) and an area can list more than sixteen. The work happens on `feature/terrain-32-layers`, branched from `feature/terrain-types`. [TerrainTypes.md](TerrainTypes.md) explains how the cap reached 16 (its Phase 1) and why the template leaves the ocean and lava out (its Phase 9 and Phase 12 notes). [TerrainBlending.md](TerrainBlending.md) describes the renderer, and [Architecture.md](Architecture.md) describes what exists.

Every rule in [Multiplayer.md](Multiplayer.md#rules-for-new-code-starting-now) applies. Ground is scene content that every machine loads the same way and draws locally. The control map, the lookup texture and the renderer's layer indices are never sent, and `AreaTerrain`'s runtime edits name a layer by its index in `AreaTerrain`'s own list. Nothing here touches replication.

## Where we start

- A cell's layers are the bits of one `R16_UNorm` texel of the control map, bit i for layer i ([TerrainControlMap](../Assets/Scripts/World/Terrain/TerrainControlMap.cs): `MaxLayers` 16, with `ushort` cells, preview bits, `GetCell` and `GetShownCell`). The shader reads a texel as `round(r * 65535)` (`TBCell` in [TerrainBlendShape.hlsl](../Assets/Shaders/Terrain/TerrainBlendShape.hlsl)), which needs full float precision.
- Two per-pixel arrays in the shader are sized by the cap: the layers' distances `d[TB_MAX_LAYERS]` and the pair weights `w[TB_MAX_LAYERS + 1]` ([TerrainBlend.hlsl](../Assets/Shaders/Terrain/TerrainBlend.hlsl)). Both are indexed by values known only at run time, so FXC declares them as indexable temporaries rather than plain registers. Unrolled loops clear the whole of each array: `d` once per pixel (`TBDistances`) and `w` once per layer drawn at the pixel (`TBPairWeights`). Every other per-layer loop already stops at the highest layer filled around the pixel (`TBLayersAt`, see TerrainTypes.md's Phase 1 notes).
- The bits of the bridge layers travel as a float in the lookup texture's shared texel (`TerrainBlendLut.BridgeBits`). A float holds a whole number exactly only up to 24 bits. The renderer and `TerrainCoastMap` keep the bits as an `int`.
- `TBNearBridgeEdge` clamps a shift to 31 ([TerrainBlendCoverage.hlsl](../Assets/Shaders/Terrain/TerrainBlendCoverage.hlsl)). That is correct only for layers below 30.
- The debug view gives each layer a pure colour whose channels are each off, at half or full, with at least one full (`TB_DEBUG_COLOURS`). Only nineteen such colours exist, and sixteen are in use. `TerrainBlendProbe.DebugClass` reads those three levels.
- The cap is enforced in four places: `TerrainControlMap`'s constructor, `TerrainBlendChecks.IsUsable`, `AreaGroundSetup.Problem` (used by the writer, Convert Ground and the Scene Generator window), and `AreaGroundSetup.SetUpLayers`, which drops layers past the cap. `TerrainBlendProbe` assumes it (`DebugLayers`).
- The area template holds sixteen layers: every material of the order but the ocean and lava (`AreaTemplateTests.LeftOut`, and the matching entry in Architecture.md's Known gaps). No generator lists more than twelve layers (the Lakeside).
- Each listed layer costs a slice in each of the albedo, cell field and height arrays (3 MiB at 512 px). The lookup texture has 1 + L + L(L + 1) rows of 37 texels of 16 bytes each: 289 rows (167 KiB) at 16 layers, 1089 rows (630 KiB) at 32. TerrainTypes.md's Phase 1 and Phase 11 notes give the measured draw cost at 16.

## Goals

- A renderer draws up to 32 layers, and the control map, the shader, the bridge bits, the debug view, the probe, every check and every message follow.
- Areas of 16 layers or fewer render byte for byte as before and draw no slower. Proof is either compiled code that does not change, or alternating measurements that stay within run-to-run noise.
- No material is a special case for the area template, the ocean and lava included: an area made from it gains any material's layer when it paints that material or a generator lists it (decided before Phase 4, see Decisions: the template grows).
- A measured answer, recorded in this file whatever it turns out to be: what the higher cap costs areas that do not use it, what a 32-layer area costs, and how much longer the shader takes to compile.

## Non-goals

- New materials, or changes to the layers any generator or area lists.
- More than 32 layers, which would need two texels or a second texture per cell.
- Support for half-precision or mobile targets beyond what the format choice gives for free.
- Restructuring the per-pixel arrays (see Open questions), unless Phase 1 shows that the cap costs every area and the keyword below is turned down.

## Decisions

- **The branch.** `feature/terrain-32-layers`, from `feature/terrain-types`. If Phase 3's numbers hold, the branch merges back into `feature/terrain-types`. If they do not, this file and its measurements are brought over on their own, and TerrainTypes.md's open question about the template links to it, so the finding survives.
- **The control map: `RGBA32` (`R8G8B8A8_UNorm`).** A cell's 32 bits are its texel's four bytes, with red as the lowest byte. They are written through `GetPixelData<uint>` and read as `r | g << 8 | b << 16 | a << 24`, with each channel read as `round(c * 255)`. 8-bit channels decode exactly even at half precision, so the R16 map's need for full floats goes away. The alternative is `RG32` (`R16G16_UNorm`): two 16-bit halves, each read like today's texel, with the same precision requirement as R16. An integer texture (`R32_UInt`) is ruled out: Shader Graph's texture inputs (`UnityTexture2D`) are float textures, so it would need a texture declared outside the graph. The map takes 4 bytes per cell instead of 2, 13.5 KiB for a 72 x 48 area. Checked in Phase 2:
  - Layout. Unity's [`GraphicsFormat.R8G8B8A8_UNorm`](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/Experimental.Rendering.GraphicsFormat.R8G8B8A8_UNorm.html) has "an 8-bit R component in byte 0, an 8-bit G component in byte 1, an 8-bit B component in byte 2, and an 8-bit A component in byte 3". [`GetPixelData<T>`](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/Texture2D.GetPixelData.html) points straight at the texture's data, so on a little-endian CPU a `uint` texel has red as its lowest byte. In the editor, `TextureFormat.RGBA32` made linear (`new Texture2D(w, h, TextureFormat.RGBA32, false, true)`) is `R8G8B8A8_UNorm`, with no sRGB decode, and a texel written as the `uint` 0x04030201 reads back as the bytes 1, 2, 3, 4 and through `GetPixel` as RGBA(1, 2, 3, 4).
  - Sampling. Direct3D requires Texture2D, `ld` and sampling for `DXGI_FORMAT_R8G8B8A8_UNORM` on [feature level 11.0 hardware](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3ddxgi/format-support-for-direct3d-11-0-feature-level-hardware) and on [12.0 hardware](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/direct3ddxgi/hardware-support-for-direct3d-12-0-formats). D3D11 and D3D12 both need 11.0 or later. Vulkan's [required format support](https://docs.vulkan.org/spec/latest/chapters/formats.html) marks every feature of `VK_FORMAT_R8G8B8A8_UNORM` as mandatory, sampled images included. `SystemInfo.IsFormatSupported(GraphicsFormat.R8G8B8A8_UNorm, GraphicsFormatUsage.Sample)` is true here under D3D12. The renderer still checks it before building wide, as it checks R16.
- **Bridge bits in two halves.** The shared texel keeps the low 16 bits in its alpha, as now, and a new shared column (`SharedHighBridgesColumn`, the first free one after the cliff columns) holds the high 16 bits. Each half is exact in a float. `BridgeBits` returns a `uint`, and the renderer and `TerrainCoastMap` store it as one. With 16 layers or fewer the new texel is empty, as it was, so the lookup texture does not change. The shader reads the high half only with `_TERRAIN_WIDE`.
- **A keyword, `_TERRAIN_WIDE` (decided in Phase 1, declared in Phase 2).** The rule: keep one shader if, with the cap at 32, every area and the 16-layer stress scene draw within run-to-run noise of the baseline and the compile test grows by at most a quarter; otherwise add the keyword. The compile test grew by about a tenth, but with the cap at 32 every area and the stress scene drew 1.8 to 2.7 times slower in crisp and 1.7 to 6.0 times slower in soft at 7680 x 4320 zoom 3 (1.35 to 2.6 times at 1920 x 1080), where runs of the same shader differ by at most 7 %, and the narrower clearing of the arrays did not bring it back (Phase 1's notes). So the shader gets a local keyword, `_TERRAIN_WIDE`, a fragment-only `multi_compile` declared in the graph like `_TERRAIN_SOFT` and set in the renderer's `ApplyMode` like `_TERRAIN_SOFT` and `_TERRAIN_RAISED`. Phase 2 declares it with the cap at 16 in both variants. Only three things go under it there: `TBCell`'s RGBA32 decode, the high half of the bridge bits, and `TBNearBridgeEdge`'s mask (below). Phase 3 then raises `TB_MAX_LAYERS` to 32 under it. This way the wide path compiles, is tested, and draws every area byte for byte before any area needs it. Without the keyword the shader compiles exactly as at the start of the branch, so the compiled code is unchanged. The cost of the keyword: twice the variants, and about twice the compile-test time (Phase 2's notes). An area of more than 16 layers runs the wide variant at every pixel, so it pays that variant's cost everywhere, not only where more than 16 layers are painted; Phase 3 measures it.
- **Narrow and wide maps.** `TerrainControlMap` is built narrow (`R16`, at most `NarrowLayers`, 16) or wide (`RGBA32`, at most `WideLayers`, 32) by an explicit choice, the `wide` argument of its constructor. Its cells, preview bits, `GetCell` and `GetShownCell` are `uint` either way. A wide map may hold 32 layers on its own, so its tests cover every bit. `MaxLayers`, the most a renderer draws, stays 16 until Phase 3, and so do `TerrainBlendChecks`, `AreaGroundSetup` and the probe. The renderer builds wide, and enables the keyword, only when it lists more than `NarrowLayers` layers, which none can before Phase 3, or when its `internal` seam `SetAlwaysWide(true)` asks. Tests use the seam to compare the wide path with the narrow one on the same layers, and Phase 2 used it to capture every area through the wide path, so no temporary code edit was needed. A wide build whose shader has no `_TERRAIN_WIDE` keyword is refused with an error, since the shader would read the RGBA32 texel as an R16 one.
- **Static arrays in the wide variant (Phase 3).** The wide variant keeps the per-pixel arrays `d` and `w` as static variables of the shader instead of passing them to every function that reads them, which made FXC copy them whole into each one. That copying was most of the cap's cost (Phase 1's notes). The narrow variant keeps the parameters, so its code does not change; macros of `TerrainBlendInputs.hlsl` name each parameter and argument, so one source serves both. Phase 3's notes give the numbers.
- **`TBNearBridgeEdge`'s mask.** It keeps the bridges at least two layers above `m`. Without the keyword it stays `1u << min(m + 2u, 31u)`. That is right for every `m` the 16-layer cap allows, and it leaves the narrow variant's code unchanged. With the keyword it is `2u << min(m + 1u, 31u)`, which never shifts by 32 or more and gives an empty mask for `m` 30 and 31. Using the new form in both variants would change the narrow variant's code, so it would have to be timed against the start of the branch. Keeping it to the wide variant is cheaper and provably the same.
- **Debug colours: layers 0 to 15 keep theirs.** Layers 16 to 31 get colours whose channels are each off, at a quarter, half, three quarters or full, with at least one channel full and none already in use. Earlier debug captures therefore stay byte for byte, and the probe reads five levels against the brightest channel. Phase 3 picks the table and checks that every two colours stay apart under the areas' light (intensity 0.85).
- **The template grows (decided before Phase 4).** Phase 3 measured the wide variant at about twice the narrow one in crisp and 1.35 times in soft, at every pixel, however few layers are painted. The renderer goes wide by the layers it lists, not by those painted. A template holding all eighteen materials would therefore make every area made from it run wide until its empty layers were removed. Areas will be both generated and painted by hand, so the template holds only the grass layer (and the collision layer). The Terrain Brush adds any other material's layer when it is picked. When the Tile Palette's picked tile is a ground material's that the area has no layer for, the brush's warning offers a button, "Add the <material> layer". The button inserts the layer at its material's place in the style's order, as one undo step, and makes it the paint target. Nothing ever moves a stroke away from the paint target the painter picked. The layer is set up as `GroundLayerMigration` sets up a generator's layers: its tilemap named after the material, the material's plain tile, the terrain type `Terrain_<material>`, its navigation source with its draw order as priority, and the renderer's entry. The generators' writer already creates the layers a generator lists, and Generate and Convert Ground already remove empty ones. An area so lists only what it uses, stays narrow until it uses more than sixteen materials, and does not pay for empty layers' texture array slices (3 MiB each at 512 px) and lookup rows.
- **The stress scene is code.** `TerrainBlendProbe.BuildStressScene(int layers)` builds the measuring scene into the active scene, so every phase and session measures the same thing. Phase 11's test scene came from a script kept in one session's scratchpad. The scene has grass over 72 x 48 cells at the origin, a white Global Light 2D at 0.85, and layers 1 to n - 1 drawing the materials of the style's order after grass in turn, starting over after the last. Each of those layers is painted as four discs of radius 2 to 5 cells, at spots drawn from a `System.Random` seeded with the layer's index. The 32-layer scene is therefore the 16-layer scene with sixteen more layers on top. Planks fall at layer 17, past 16, so the scene exercises the high bridge bits.

## How to measure

The method follows TerrainTypes.md's Phases 1 and 11, plus what later sessions learned:

- **Cost:** `TerrainBlendProbe.MeasureCost` in crisp and soft at 7680 x 4320 zoom 2, 7680 x 4320 zoom 3 and 1920 x 1080 zoom 3, taking the best of three runs of 20 frames and noting the chunks-hidden time. Time old and new alternately in one session, twice each, by swapping the shader files and refreshing. Never compare against numbers from another session. Measure with the editor unfocused; no script may bring Unity to the front.
- **Compile time:** `ShaderData.Pass.CompileVariant` for each Sprite Lit fragment variant (crisp and soft, each with and without `_TERRAIN_RAISED`), with an empty scene open, plus the `ShaderCompile` test. Compare the compiled bytes per variant (the DXBC after Unity's 38-byte header) to show when a change compiles to the same code.
- **Byte for byte:** capture each of the six areas, opened alone, over the control map's bounds at one screen pixel per art pixel: both looks at fixed times of 1.5 s and 2.75 s, plus the debug view, plus `CheckBlobs` in both looks. Capture the stress scene the same way. Keep the hashes in the session's scratchpad; the phase notes record how many files were compared and whether they matched.
- **Rebuild and memory:** `MeasureRebuildAndModeSwitch`, best of five, and the sizes of the arrays and the lookup texture, read back from the built renderer.

- **Why the cap costs every pixel.** `d` and `w` are passed by value, and FXC gives every function they are passed to a copy of its own: seven arrays for `d` (x0 to x6) and nine for `w` (x7 to x15). It reads each array once into registers and writes it, whole, into each copy where the function is inlined: `d`, filled by `TBDistances`, into two copies once per pixel, into one on every step of the loop that finds the opaque start layer, and into three for each layer drawn (for `TBNearBridgeEdge`, `TBVisible` and `TBPairWeights`); `w`, cleared and built by `TBPairWeights` for each layer drawn, into eight (for `TBGetEdge`, `TBLayer` and the functions it calls). Each element of the cap is therefore 18 moves in the code (`d`: its clearing, the read and six copies; `w`: its clearing, the read and eight copies), which makes the whole of the 288 instructions from 16 to 32, and the eight extra temps are the registers that hold the 32 more values on their way. Every copy is as long as the cap, whatever the area lists or paints, so every area pays for it. The soft variants, which already use 166 to 172 temps against 106 to 111 in crisp, slow down most; whether the driver then keeps the arrays in registers or spills them to memory does not show in the DXBC.

### Phase 2: a 32-bit control map

- **The decision** (Decisions: the template grows) was taken with the numbers of Phase 3 in hand. The button was chosen over moving a stroke to the right layer automatically, so a stroke always lands on the paint target the painter picked. - **Adding a layer.** `GroundLayerMigration.AddLayer(grid, layer)` checks the grid, the style and the cap, then calls `AreaGroundSetup.InsertLayer`. It then applies the plan with the second half of `SetUpLayers`, now `ApplyPlan`, which `SetUpLayers` still uses. The steps are recorded in the order the writer records them: the new tilemap, the moves among the grid's children, the navigation sources and priorities, then the renderer, collapsed into one undo group named "Add <material> layer". `Migrate` could not do it, since it removes empty layers a generator does not list. `AddLayer(grid, material)` finds the project's tile (`Assets/Tiles/Ground/Tile_<material>`) and terrain type (`Assets/Settings/AI/Terrain_<material>`) by name, as the generators' layers and the template's did. The palette follower then selects that material's palette (it already does so when the paint target changes). The renderer rebuilds after the editor's update, as for any inspector change to its layers, and draws the layer from then on.

## Open questions

- **Arrays of only the layers present.** If the cap costs every area and the keyword is turned down, `d` and `w` could hold only the few layers present at a pixel, as a short list built from `TBLayersAt`'s bits, so per-pixel cost no longer depends on the cap at all. This is a much larger shader change; do it only if needed.
- **Arrays that are not copied, in the narrow variant too.** Phase 3 made `d` and `w` static variables in the wide variant (its notes), which left one indexable array of each, cut a 32-layer area's cost to 0.61 times in crisp and 0.21 times in soft, and made it hardly depend on the layers painted. The narrow variant still passes them as parameters, so its code stays as it was. Doing the same there would drop the macros and might make the soft look faster, and the Coast's soft look, which draws 1.8 times slower than its crisp look (Phase 1's notes). But built wide, the 16-layer stress scene drew 12 % slower in crisp with static arrays than with copies, so the crisp look could lose. Timing it needs the alternated measurements of every area against the start of the branch.
- **A template that grows instead.** Chosen for Phase 4 (Decisions: the template grows).
