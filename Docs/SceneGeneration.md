# Scene generation

An editor tool that fills a standalone scene with generated content: terrain layers (plain data tilemaps drawn by the grid's blended terrain, see [TerrainBlending.md](TerrainBlending.md)), props and mob spawn points. The result is saved in the scene like hand-made content. There are six generators: the forest Clearing, the Village Forest, which joins a village, a clearing and a forest in one larger area, the Lakeside, which shows most terrain types around a lake, the Snowfield, a field of snow around a frozen lake (Phase 7 of TerrainTypes.md), the Coast, a beach giving onto the ocean with a plateau of raised grass (Phases 8 and 10), and the Lava Fields, scorched earth crossed by a lava river (Phase 9).

**No area scenes.** Each generator made an area scene of its own (`Area_Clearing.unity` and so on), played one at a time under the Gameplay scene. Seamless worlds replaced that path, and the six area scenes, their `SceneDefinition`s and the area template went in [WorldStreaming.md](WorldStreaming.md)'s Phase 9. The generator assets keep their settings and seed 1, so world generation ([WorldGeneration.md](WorldGeneration.md)) brings the six layouts back as regions of a world: its `WorldGenerationWriter` (Phase 2) writes a composed world, the regions' cells, raised ground, mobs and props among it, into the world's chunk scenes, while `AreaLayoutWriter` and the Scene Generator still write into scratch scenes only and refuse a chunk's grid. The Overworld, the first generated world, comes in WorldGeneration.md's Phase 4; until then every world's chunks are painted by hand. The numbers below are each generator asset's layout for seed 1, with its props.

**Natural props, no built ones.** The generators place the natural props generated in the editor ([PropGeneration.md](PropGeneration.md)), as its Phase 5 set them:
- Every scatter layer of the six assets lists all four variants of the looks of its terrain (that plan's Natural props per terrain). A look listed more than once is picked as many times as often.
- The edge bands (the forest edges, the Snowfield's pines, the Lava Fields' scorched edge) take a spacing of 1.3. A trunk blocks one cell and layouts are in whole cells, so any spacing from 1.42 to 2 lays the same edge, with no two trunks touching even diagonally; at 1.3 they may stand diagonally next to each other and close into a band. Every other layer keeps its spacing.
- After their tall grass, the Lakeside dries the bushes standing wholly on its dry grass or tall grass and the Village Forest those on its tall grass. Each `Bush_Green` or `Bush_Flowering` there becomes a `Bush_Dry` covering the same cells, picked by its cell's hash (`AreaLayoutBuilder.SwapProps` with the generator's `dryBushes`), so nothing else moves.
- Props stand on the ground of their zones, never on a path, the water, the sand or the mud: the shore looks stand on the grass along the beaches and the marsh (props on the sand and the mud are that plan's Later).
- The assets set no building, fence, fountain or town props (built props are that plan's Non-goals). Since [Lighting.md](Lighting.md)'s Phase 5 they set the light props: a campfire, with the region's own stumps as seats, for the Clearing, the Snowfield, the Coast and the Lava Fields, so their campsites are laid, and a lamp post for the Village Forest's street. The Village Forest lays its square only with a fountain and its buildings, with their lots, doorstep paths, fences and yards, only with a building, so no square or building is laid.

The steps below describe what each generator does with all its props set. `AreaGenerationTests` runs them that way, with test built props (`TestPropPrefabs`) beside the assets' scatter props. The numbers after the steps are each asset's own, with its props: seed 1, then the ranges over twenty seeds. World generation lays the regions with their props and writes them into the world's chunks (PropGeneration.md's Phase 6), but for a blocking prop whose footprint would cross a chunk's border and the props a waystone's plaza covers.

## Using it

1. Make a scene to generate into: **New scratch scene** in the Scene Generator window (step 2), after offering to save the open scenes, makes an unsaved scene with a Global Light 2D and a standalone grid at the origin set up like a world chunk's (`WorldAuthoring.CreateScratchScene`): one empty ground layer, the grass, drawn by a `TerrainBlendRenderer`, a `NavigationGrid2D`, an `AreaTerrain` listing the grass layer and the walls, and the walls from `Walls.prefab`. Any scene whose active grid has no `WorldChunk` works too; a world chunk's grid is refused, since writing layouts into worlds belongs to world generation. A grid gains the other layers as it uses them: Generate and Convert Ground create the generator's layers (see below), and the Terrain Brush adds a material's layer when its tile is picked in the Tile Palette (TerrainLayers32.md's Phase 4), so a grid lists only what it uses.
2. Open **Tools > TopDownRPG > World > Scene Generator** and pick a generator asset: [Generator_Clearing.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_Clearing.asset), [Generator_VillageForest.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_VillageForest.asset), [Generator_Lakeside.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_Lakeside.asset), [Generator_Snowfield.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_Snowfield.asset), [Generator_Coast.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_Coast.asset) or [Generator_LavaFields.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_LavaFields.asset). The scratch scene's `AreaTerrain` lists its grass layer; for a generator whose ground is not grass (the Snowfield's snow, the Lava Fields' dirt), point that entry at the generator's first layer before generating, or the empty grass layer is kept, since `AreaTerrain` lists it.
3. Tune its settings in the window. A map of the layout redraws on every change, one pixel per cell: each terrain layer in its material's base colour (a shade darker when a layer below it has the same colour), blocking footprints dark green, walk-through props light green, mob spawns red, the spawn cyan. The line under it counts cells, props and mobs, the next the props each scatter layer placed, by label, and warnings say what did not fit. When the generator's layers cannot be set up (no terrain style, more than 16 layers, a layer missing its tilemap name, tile or material, or layers that are not a subsequence of the style's layer order), an error box says why and Generate and Convert Ground are disabled.
4. **Random Seed** picks a new seed. The seed is stored on the generator asset, and the same seed and settings always give the same layout.
5. **Generate** asks first, then replaces the scene's content as a single undo step. Review the scene and save it.
6. **Convert Ground** asks first, then sets the scene's ground layers up as the generator's layers without generating anything (see `GroundLayerMigration` below), as a single undo step: every painted cell stays, and props, mobs and walls are left alone. Use it after changing a generator's terrain layers (a tile, a material, a layer added or dropped) on a scene you do not want to regenerate: it creates the layers the area lacks, empty, keeps a painted layer the generator no longer lists (with a warning) and removes an empty one. Phase 4 of TerrainBlending.md converted every area's dual-grid ground with it, Phase 5 gave the Clearing and the Village Forest their empty sand, mud and water layers, and Phase 2 of TerrainTypes.md trimmed both to the layers they use.

Terrain a generator lays after its props and mobs (the Clearing's campsite and weeds, the Village Forest's dirt, tall grass, meadows and weeds, the Lakeside's dry grass, shallow ponds, tall grass, meadows and weeds, the Snowfield's holes and campsite, the Coast's campsite, plateau and weeds and the Lava Fields' campsite) draws from a second random stream (the Lakeside's dock, laid between the ponds and the tall grass, draws nothing) seeded from the same seed (`AreaLayoutBuilder.StartDetailStream`), so adding such a step left every path, prop and mob of the earlier layouts where it was.

What Generate replaces:
- Every terrain layer the generator lists: the data tilemap child of the scene's `Grid` with that name is cleared and filled with the layer's plain tile in exactly the layout's cells. A layer the scene does not have yet is created as a plain tilemap. Each layer is left a plain data tilemap: a dual-grid module, any tilemap under it (its render tilemap, even one left behind without its module) or a Tilemap Renderer still on it is removed, since every cell is repainted anyway. Its navigation source (added when missing) gets the layer's terrain type and its index among the layers drawn as priority, and the layers drawn are put first among the grid's children, in draw order.
- Any other layer the renderer drew, one the generator does not list: kept, with its cells and its material, when it is painted or the grid's `AreaTerrain` lists it, at its material's place in the style's layer order (above everything, when the order does not list its material), with a warning naming it; removed when empty (its tilemap, a child of the grid, is deleted).
- The grid's `TerrainBlendRenderer`, added when missing: it draws exactly those layers, in the shared order (its draw order, and so the navigation priorities), with their materials and the generator's terrain style; past 32 layers (`TerrainControlMap.MaxLayers`) the kept ones from the top are dropped with a warning. Entries without a tilemap are dropped. Its other settings (chunk size, sorting, look) stay.
- The fill tile of each of the generator's layers that the grid's `AreaTerrain` lists: the layer's tile.
- For a generator with raised ground (a raised tile, `AreaGenerator.RaisedTile`), the grid's `RaisedTilemap` (created when missing): cleared and filled with the raised tile in the layout's raised cells and the ramp tile in its ramps, and set on the grid's terrain renderer, which draws it, and its navigation grid, which stops units at its cliff. Convert Ground sets the tilemap up the same way without repainting it (`AreaGroundSetup.SetUpRaised`).
- Every child of the `Props` root, so props placed by hand there go too.
- Every `MobSpawnPoint` in the scene. The new ones go under a `MobSpawns` root, named `MobSpawn_<prefab>_<n>`.

The layout's spawn cell is written nowhere: it only shapes the layout (paths, clear ground, the mobs' distances), and the preview shows it. The collision tilemap (walls), the rest of `AreaTerrain`, lights and any other objects are left alone. Generated layouts assume cells of one unit, like every world; the writer refuses any other grid.

### Speed

A generation reads sets of cells, the ground's edge distances and the props' spacing many times per cell, so those are kept cheap; the times below are in the editor, without props unless they say so.
- **Cell hashing.** Unity's `Vector3Int` hash mixes x with y shifted by four bits, so an area's few thousand cells share about a thousand hash codes and each lookup walks a chain of several. Every `HashSet<Vector3Int>` and `Dictionary<Vector3Int, T>` of generation, and of `AreaGenerationTests`, takes `CellComparer.Instance`, whose hash multiplies each coordinate by its own large prime. The order a set gives its cells in is the order they went in, whatever the hash, so no layout changed.
- **Edge distances** live in an array over the ground's bounds (`AreaLayoutBuilder.EdgeDistance`), filled by a breadth-first pass from the cells beside missing ground, rather than in a dictionary.
- **Prop spacing.** A scatter layer keeps the points it has placed bucketed in squares a little larger than its spacing, so a try reads the nine squares around it instead of every prop placed: the Village Forest's forest trees, which ask for 2000, went from 30 ms to a few.
- **The Village Forest's forest edge** for tall grass comes from summed-area tables of the forest's and the other regions' cells over the regions' bounds, a few array reads per cell instead of a lookup per cell of a 7 x 7 square.

Together they took the six generators' twenty seeds from 7.4 s to 5.3 s without props and from 9.7 s to 6.7 s with props like the ones they were built with (the Village Forest from 3.8 s to 1.9 s), and `AreaGenerationTests` from about 42 s to 26 s. Each generator's twenty layouts, with and without those props, were compared before and after, cell by cell and in their sets' order, and are the same.

With the generated props (PropGeneration.md's Phase 5), the best of three runs, alternating with and without them:

## The Clearing generator

One generation, in order:

1. **Grass**: the cells whose centres lie inside a superellipse of `size` (`squareness` 2 is an ellipse, higher is squarer). Its radius wobbles by up to `edgeWobble` with four random harmonics around it. Only the largest 4-connected region is kept. Every grass cell records its distance in steps (8 neighbours) to the nearest cell without grass.
2. **Stone road**: a Catmull-Rom spline through five points from beyond the west edge to beyond the east edge, each at a random height within `roadMeander` of the middle. Every grass cell whose centre lies within half of `roadWidth` of the curve is painted. A road cell outside the grass is dropped, so paths always lie on grass.
3. **Gravel paths**: `gravelPathCount` branches leave the middle half of the road, alternating north and south, and run out past the edge through a point that bends them sideways.
4. **Spawn**: on the road where it passes `spawnAlongRoad` (from -1 for the west edge to 1 for the east edge), or at the nearest road cell that has grass all around it for `spawnClearRadius` cells. If no road cell has that room, the spawn goes to the deepest grass cell, with a warning. Both generators share this rule.
5. **Open ground**: blocking props are kept off the path cells and `pathClearance` cells beside them, which leaves a gap in the forest edge where a path leaves, and off the disc of `spawnClearRadius` around the spawn.
6. **Scatter layers**, in list order, so earlier layers take the room first. Each one tries the cells of its zone in random order: `Border` is the band `borderDepth` cells deep along the edge, `Interior` is everything inside it. Each try takes a random prefab from the layer, and stops once `count` props are placed. A prefab with a footprint collider is a blocking prop. Its footprint stands on the tried cell, as centred as its width allows, and every cell it covers must be free grass. A walk-through prop goes at a random point in its cell, which must be free and not next to a path. Every prop also keeps `spacing` from the other props of its layer.
7. **Mobs**: `mobCount` spawns of `mobPrefab` on cells inside the border band that the spawn can walk to (4-connected around the footprints, like the navigation grid's regions). Each one is away from props, at least `mobDistanceFromSpawn` from the spawn and `mobSpacing` from the other mobs. A warning reports any that did not fit.
8. **Campsite**, from the detail stream (`AreaLayoutBuilder.LayCampsite`, shared with the Snowfield): a centre on interior grass deeper than the edge band plus `campsiteRadius`, with no path within that radius plus the path clearance, its radius clear of the spawn's disc and of every mob; among such cells, one with the fewest cells under blocking props within the radius less half a cell, where the fire and the seats go. The props in that circle are removed, a wobbly patch of dirt of the radius is laid around the centre (on grass off the paths), and the plants on or beside it go too, since a plant's sprite stands above its cell. `campfirePrefab` stands in the middle and `seatCount` of `campsiteSeats` on the dirt between 1.5 cells and the radius less half a cell from it, `seatSpacing` apart. A tree at the patch's rim stays and stands on the dirt. Props are only removed, so nothing else moves and the spawn reaches all it reached before. A warning reports a clearing with no such cell; none of 20 seeds has one.
9. **Weeds**, last, on the detail stream (Phase 12 of TerrainTypes.md; `AreaLayoutBuilder.LayWeeds` with the generator's `weedPatches`): wobbly patches of 2 to 3.5 cells on the plain grass, every cell of them two cells or more inside the map's edge with no cell of another layer within a step, so a patch meets only the grass, which shares its ground: first the edge count, centred on and kept to cells within `reach` (3) of the band of trees, then the open count anywhere; pieces under four cells are dropped. Nothing laid before them moves. The detail stream now starts in `Build`, before the campsite, which draws from it as before.

All randomness comes from a `System.Random` seeded with the seed (the campsite from the second stream), and every loop walks ordered lists rather than hash sets, so a seed always gives the same layout. A generation takes about 11 ms with its props and 9 ms without (see Speed).

The Clearing lists grass, dirt, gravel and stone. Its asset has four scatter layers and a campsite of three seats on dirt 3 cells in radius, its campfire `Campfire_0` and its seats the oak stumps (Lighting.md's Phase 5):
- a forest edge, up to 280 at a spacing of 1.3: `Tree_Broadleaf` twice as often as `Tree_BroadleafSmall`;
- trees, 12 at 5 inside: the same mix;
- bushes and rocks, 10 at 5: `Bush_Green` and `Rock_Grey` two shares each, `Bush_Flowering`, `Boulder_Grey`, `Stump_Oak` and `Log_Oak` one each;
- plants, 45 at 2.2: `Fern` three shares, `Stones_Small` two, `Mushrooms_Brown` and `Mushrooms_Red` one each.

Seed 1 places 160, 12, 10 and 45 of them (227 props, 182 blocking); the twenty seeds 142 to 162 edge trees and every other layer its count, 209 to 229 props. Seed 1 lays 1301 cells of grass, a road of 149 cells and gravel paths of 75, no dirt, and three mobs, then 97 cells of weeds (4 edge and 3 open patches); the twenty seeds lay 83 to 124. Until Phase 3 of TerrainTypes.md the Clearing also listed sand, mud and water, generated empty for a pond painted by hand in its scene; the pond went when the area was regenerated, and with it the layers.

### What the PlayMode tests rely on

The PlayMode tests that needed a session booted into the Clearing's scene until WorldStreaming.md's Phase 9; they now boot into Testlands, the hand-painted test world, at its `start` entry, which keeps the Clearing's margins (`TestlandsPlayModeTests` checks them). Testlands is no longer the game's starting world (the Overworld is, at `clearing`, since WorldGeneration.md's Phase 5), so `SceneBootTestHelper` gives every boot a copy of `GameScenes` starting at Testlands' `start`: the tests that start from the menu (New Game, Continue, Host Game, Join Game), the in-process host and client, which resolve `GameScenes` from the booted scopes, and a joining player the host does not know all start there, as before. Only `OverworldPlayModeTests`' two menu cases boot with the project's own starting world. `PlayerControlsPlayModeTests` builds a seven-cell wall two cells right of the spawn through its chunk's `AreaTerrain` and walks around it, and also needs five clear cells in a straight line beside it: every cell within 6 of `start` is open ground. Mobs spawn at least 14 cells from the spawn (Testlands' are 23 or more away), more than twice their detection radius, and the tests that watch one mob deactivate the others. Keep these margins when repainting Testlands around `start`, or adjust the tests. The Clearing generator keeps `spawnClearRadius` 6 and its mobs 14 cells from its spawn, so a region generated from it would give the same room.

`AreaGenerationTests` checks over 20 seeds that each of the six assets fills every scatter layer with props of the props' folder, places some in each layer for every seed, places nothing else but its own light props and their seats (a campfire at every seed where it lays a campsite, the Village Forest's lamp posts) and warns of nothing. It checks over 20 seeds that the Clearing's paths lie on grass with the spawn on the road and its disc open, footprints on grass and apart, every mob reachable and spread out, and a campsite on a patch of dirt on the grass, off the paths, outside the spawn's disc and deeper than the edge band, with one campfire and every seat on the dirt and no plant on or beside it; and, for each generator, that a copy with its detail-stream step turned off lays every other layer, mob, the spawn and every prop the same (the campsite's props and those it replaced aside).

## The Village Forest generator

One larger area, ringed by forest, that joins three environments: a village of cottages along a stone street that enters from the map edge, an open clearing, and a dense forest crossed by winding gravel trails, with tall grass across the forest's edge and flower meadows in the clearing. One generation, in order:

1. **Grass**: as in the Clearing, from `size`, `squareness` and `edgeWobble`. The asset's 92 x 62 with squareness 4 is nearly rectangular.
2. **Regions**: the village is a blob of `villageSize` against the west or east edge (picked at random), moved inwards by up to three cells; the clearing is a blob of `clearingSize` on the other side of the middle, off to the north or south. Both are superellipses whose radius wobbles by `regionWobble`. Every grass cell in the band `borderDepth` deep along the edge, and every cell outside both blobs, is forest, so the forest rings the whole area and fills the rest.
3. **Street**: stone, `streetWidth` wide: a spline through four points from beyond the village's edge of the map, through the village's middle row, to the village's inner end, the junction. Each point is within `streetMeander` cells of that row. Keep the meander small: the cottages line up along the street.
4. **Spawn**: on the street at `spawnAlongStreet` (0 at the map edge, 1 at the junction), or the nearest point along it with grass all around for `spawnClearRadius` cells. The disc around it is kept free of blocking props from here on.
5. **Square**: a disc of plaza of `squareRadius` opening south off the street at `squareAlongStreet`, around a bed of grass that holds the fountain's footprint with `pathClearance` cells around it, so the fountain keeps its clearance from the paving like any blocking prop. The disc touches the street, which is drawn over the plaza where they meet; any street cell inside it is paved too, under the stone. The plaza counts as a path for every later step, as stone did.
6. **Buildings**: walking the street from the map edge towards the junction, a lot is tried at every column, and after one fits the next starts `lotGap` columns further on. A lot is the building's width plus `sideYard` columns on each side; from the street up it holds a row of clearance, the fence row, `yardDepth` rows of yard, the building, then `backYard` rows. The Cottage's sprite shows its front wall and porch, so its front is its south side and it faces the street. Every column of a lot must border the street, the lot must be grass off the paths, the edge band and the clearing, the building must stay out of the spawn's disc, and the lot must end `LotMargin` columns (half the trail width plus the clearance plus one) before the junction, where the trails start. A one-cell stone path runs from the street up to the doorstep, the cell in front of the door: `doorColumn` counts from the footprint's left, and 3 puts the Cottage's doorstep under its porch. The doorstep touches the building, so it is the one path cell allowed within a building's clearance. Every cell of a built lot joins the village, even where the village's wobbly outline fell short of it. A warning reports fewer than `buildingCount` buildings.
7. **Trails**: gravel, `trailWidth` wide. The first runs from the junction to the clearing's middle and on across it, away from the village. Then come `forestTrailCount` forest trails. Each aims at a forest cell at least `borderDepth + gladeRadius + 1` from the edge, picked at random among the eighth of them furthest from every path laid so far, and starts from the nearest point of the trails already laid. A trail bends about every nine cells, each bend pushed sideways by up to `trailWinding` of the distance between bends, unless that would take it into the edge band. A trail that would enter the village, come within `LotMargin` of a lot or leave the grass is dropped, the cells around its target are ruled out, and another target is tried, up to 24 times. Each forest trail ends in a glade, a disc of `gladeRadius` kept free of blocking props. Every trail starts on a path, so all the paths form one network. A path cell that touches that network only at a corner, left where the edge of the grass clipped a path, is then dropped.
8. **Open ground**: blocking props are kept off every path and the `pathClearance` cells beside it, and off the glades.
9. **Village furniture**: the fountain in the middle of the square; a fence along each lot's front row, laid from either side of the gate outwards; `townPropsPerBuilding` town props in each yard, in front of and beside its building, and `townPropsAroundSquare` in a ring south of the square, each set `townPropSpacing` apart. The lots are then reserved, so the scatter layers put no blocking prop in a yard.
10. **Scatter layers**, in list order, each over the grass cells of its region, placed as in the Clearing.
11. **Mobs**: each region's count of `mobPrefab` (the clearing's, then the forest's, then the village's) on cells of that region the spawn can walk to, deeper than the edge band, not next to a blocking prop, at least `mobDistanceFromSpawn` from the spawn and `mobSpacing` from every other mob. A warning reports a region where fewer fit.
12. **Reachability**: once every prop stands, a warning names any path cell, doorstep or region the spawn cannot walk to (4-connected around the footprints, like the navigation grid's regions). The dense forest can seal off a few stray cells between trees, which matters to nothing; the paths, doors, mobs and regions are what is checked.
13. **Dirt**, from the detail stream: shoulders beside the gravel trails near the village. Each trail cell within `dirtReach` of the junction gives a width, `dirtWidth` at the junction falling to nothing at the reach; a grass cell off the paths and the lots is dirt when it lies within a trail cell's width, moved in and out by up to `dirtWobble` cells by three smooth waves 5 to 9 cells long at random angles. Dirt may run under a tree beside a trail; props stand on it as on grass.
14. **Tall grass**, on the detail stream after the dirt: `tallGrassCount` wobbly patches, radius between the two values of `tallGrassRadius`, each centred on a random cell of the forest's edge band where tall grass may grow and none lies yet. The band is every grass cell with a cell of the other side within `tallGrassReach` (a clearing or village cell for a forest cell, a forest cell for the others), so a patch straddles the edge, under the first trees and out onto the open ground. Tall grass keeps a cell from the paths, the dirt and the lots and two cells inside the map's edge, so it meets only grass and no third look shows under its rounded corners (TerrainTypes.md's Phase 3 notes); pieces of fewer than four cells are dropped. Trees stand in it; the plants (walk-through props) on it are removed, since a mushroom or a fern drawn over its blades would stand on top of them.
15. **Meadows**, on the detail stream after the tall grass: `meadowCount` wobbly patches, radius between the two values of `meadowRadius`, on clearing cells two inside the map's edge and a cell from the paths, the dirt and the tall grass, each centred on a random such cell, no meadow yet, whose cells within two steps are all such cells. Props stand on them as on grass.
16. **Weeds**, on the detail stream (Phase 12 of TerrainTypes.md; `AreaLayoutBuilder.LayWeeds` with the generator's `weedPatches`): wobbly patches of 2 to 3.5 cells on the plain grass, every cell of them two cells or more inside the map's edge with no cell of another layer within a step, so a patch meets only the grass, which shares its ground: first the edge count, centred on and kept to cells within `reach` (3) of the band of trees, then the open count anywhere; pieces under four cells are dropped. Nothing laid before them moves.
17. **Lamp posts**, last, on the detail stream ([Lighting.md](Lighting.md)'s Phase 5): about every `lampSpacing` cells along the street through the village, alternately north and south of it just beyond its clearance and outside the lots, so the northern ones stand in the gaps between yards rather than in their fences; each only where no blocking prop or mob stands, taking the walk-through props off its cells, so nothing laid before them moves.

## The Coast generator

A coast (Phase 8 of TerrainTypes.md): grass ringed by trees, with a sandy beach along its northern edge giving onto the ocean, which runs out to the map's edge, a stone road across the south, a gravel trail from the road onto the beach and a campsite on trodden dirt. The sea is in the north so that the beach faces down the screen and the ocean's waves, which travel south, run towards it; a sunken layer shows its bank only along edges that face up the screen, so the coast shows none, as the Lakeside's beach on the lake's southern shore shows none. One generation, in order:

1. **Grass**: as in the Clearing, from `size`, `squareness` and `edgeWobble`. The asset's 64 x 46 with squareness 6 is nearly rectangular, so the sea runs most of the way along the northern edge before the corners cut it.
2. **Coast**: two smooth lines across the area, each a sum of three sine waves of random wavelengths between the two values of `coastWavelength` and random phases. Cells north of the first, `oceanDepth` rows below the area's nominal northern edge give or take `oceanWobble`, are ocean (only its largest 4-connected piece; a piece cut off where the outline steps at a corner is beach); the next band, between the two values of `beachWidth` rows wide, is beach (sand). 3. **Stone road**: as the Snowfield's, around `roadSouth` of the half height south of the middle, wandering by `roadMeander`, never on the coast; a warning counts road cells within `roadMargin` of it.

It leaves the road where the road's northern edge runs level across it, goes straight north for three cells, bends once, and crosses the beach's landward edge straight north too, so it meets neither the road nor the beach's edge at a step (a step there leaves the lower one's edge running on under the trail's rounded corner). No trail cell lies on or beside the ocean: it ends on the sand with a row of beach between it and the water.
5. **Spawn**: on the road at `spawnAlongRoad`, as in the Clearing, with its disc of `spawnClearRadius` kept free of blocking props.
6. **Open ground**: blocking props are kept off the paths and the `pathClearance` cells beside them, the spawn's disc, the beach and the ocean.
7. **Scatter layers**, in list order, over the grass off the beach and the ocean. `Border` is the edge band `borderDepth` deep, `Shore` the grass within `shoreZoneDepth` of the beach, `Interior` the rest.
8. **Mobs**: `mobCount` spawns on land inside the border band that the spawn can walk to around the footprints and the ocean, away from props, at least `mobDistanceFromSpawn` from the spawn and `mobSpacing` apart.
9. **Reachability**: a warning counts any beach or trail cell the spawn cannot walk to.
10. **Campsite**, from the detail stream: the Clearing's campsite (`AreaLayoutBuilder.LayCampsite`), its centre further from the beach, the ocean and the paths than its radius plus two cells, so its dirt meets only grass.
11. **Plateau**, on the detail stream after the campsite (Phase 10 of TerrainTypes.md; `AreaLayoutBuilder.LayPlateau`): raised grass on grass cells more than `plateauMargin` from the coast, the paths and the campsite's dirt, outside the spawn's disc and that margin, and a cell from every mob. It is centred on a random cell whose cells within `plateauSize` (half cells) all qualify, or four fifths, two thirds or half of it when none has room: a main block of 0.8 to 1 times that size with one or two blocks of 0.4 to 0.7 of it set on its sides, then every cell not in a run of three across and in one down taken off until none is left, and its largest piece kept, so it has long straight cliffs and the cliff all round it leaves a top to walk on. Its ramp, `rampWidth` cells, lies in the middle of the run of bottom cells (the cell below not raised) nearest the plateau's middle that is at least two cells longer, so a cliff stands on either side, with two rows of walkable ground (no ocean) in front of it; a plateau without one is laid again round another centre, up to eight times. The props on its face (the bottom cells and the rows above them the style's cliff height reaches into, two at 64 px), the ramp, as many rows above it and the four rows in front of it a column wider are removed, and so is every footprint that lies both on and off it; props on its top stay. A warning says when no plateau fits, and when its cliff cuts the spawn off from the ramp, the top above it, the beach, the trail or a mob.
12. **Weeds**, last, on the detail stream (Phase 12 of TerrainTypes.md; `AreaLayoutBuilder.LayWeeds` with the generator's `weedPatches`): wobbly patches of 2 to 3.5 cells on the plain grass, every cell of them two cells or more inside the map's edge with no cell of another layer within a step, so a patch meets only the grass, which shares its ground: first the edge count, centred on and kept to cells within `reach` (3) of the band of trees, then the open count anywhere; pieces under four cells are dropped. Nothing laid before them moves. They may lie on the plateau, whose cliff is drawn over them.

The ocean cannot be walked and is drawn over everything under it, so it decides its cells: no unit walks onto the sea or off the map's northern edge. The beach is walked like grass. The plateau's cliff is a line between its cells and the level ground around them that the navigation grid's raised tilemap stops every step across but the ramp's foot (`RaisedGround.CanCross`; TerrainTypes.md's Phase 13), so its top is reached only over the ramp, while its rim (`RaisedGround.IsCliff`: every raised cell beside level ground but the ramp) is walked like the rest of its top, but for the two rows of its front its 64 px cliff face covers whole, which are blocked except above the ramp, where the ramp's slope rises (`RaisedGround.IsOnFace`; TerrainTypes.md's Phase 14). A generation takes about 46 ms with its props and 43 ms without. The asset ([Generator_Coast.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_Coast.asset), seed 1) has an ocean 9 rows deep give or take 2 and a beach 4 to 7 rows wide along lines of waves 12 to 36 cells long, the road 5 cells or more from the coast, the Clearing's campsite (3 seats, on dirt 3 cells in radius) without its campfire and seats, so no campsite is laid, the Clearing's mob (four Weasels) and the Lakeside's kinds of scatter layers:

## The Lava Fields generator

Lava fields (Phase 9 of TerrainTypes.md): scorched earth ringed by dead trees and dark rock, crossed from north to south by a river of lava with pools beside it, a stone road across the south that crosses the river on a strip of earth where the river stops short of it, a gravel trail from the road towards the lava and a campsite. Dirt is its ground, the layer the builder lays the outline in and every other layer lies on, as snow is the Snowfield's; it lists no grass, and the area's `AreaTerrain` points at its dirt layer. One generation, in order:

1. **Earth**: as the Clearing's grass, from `size`, `squareness` and `edgeWobble`. The asset's 64 x 46.
2. **Stone road**: as the Snowfield's, around `roadSouth` of the half height south of the middle, wandering by `roadMeander`.
3. **Spawn**: on the road at `spawnAlongRoad`, as in the Clearing, with its disc of `spawnClearRadius` kept free of lava and blocking props.
4. **Lava**: a river `riverWidth` wide through five points from beyond the northern edge to beyond the southern one, around a course up to `riverOffset` of the half width east or west of the middle, each point wandering by up to `riverMeander`; then `poolCount` wobbly pools of a radius between the two values of `poolRadius`, centred on earth inside the border band near the river. Lava keeps a cell of earth from every path (no path within a step, 8 neighbours) and stays out of the spawn's disc and the cell beyond it, so the river stops short of the road on both sides and the road crosses it on earth; pieces of fewer than four cells are dropped, and an island of earth the lava encloses (fewer than 12 cells, off the map's edge) is filled. The earth goes on under the lava.
5. **Trail**: gravel, `trailWidth` wide, from the road to earth a cell from the lava, six rows or more north of the road and inside the border band: the ends are tried nearest a random point of the northern half first, the trail leaving the road straight north where its edge runs level (`AreaLayoutBuilder.TrailStart`, the Coast's) and bending once by an offset drawn once, and the first end whose whole trail keeps a cell of earth from the lava is taken, so the trail runs unbroken and stops short of the lava (gravel beside the lava with only earth under it would show the lava under its rounded corners). A warning says when none fits.
6. **Open ground**: blocking props are kept off the paths and the `pathClearance` cells beside them, the spawn's disc, the lava and the earth beside it.
7. **Scatter layers**, in list order, over the earth off the lava and the cells beside it. `Border` is the edge band `borderDepth` deep, `Shore` the earth within `shoreZoneDepth` of the lava, `Interior` the rest.
8. **Mobs**: `mobCount` spawns on earth two steps or more from the lava inside the border band that the spawn can walk to around the footprints and the lava, away from props, at least `mobDistanceFromSpawn` from the spawn and `mobSpacing` apart.
9. **Reachability**: a warning counts any trail cell the spawn cannot walk to.
10. **Campsite**, from the detail stream: the Clearing's campsite (`AreaLayoutBuilder.LayCampsite`), its centre further from the lava and the paths than its radius plus two cells. The earth is its own trodden ground, so its patch is cleared of props and plants but not painted.

Lava cannot be walked and is drawn over everything under it, so it decides its cells; the earth, the road and the trail are walked like grass. A generation takes about 42 ms with its props and 39 ms without. The asset ([Generator_LavaFields.asset](../Assets/Settings/Generation/Generator_LavaFields.asset), seed 1) has a river 3 cells wide up to 0.15 of the half width off the middle wandering by 0.25, five pools of 2 to 3.5 cells, the Clearing's campsite settings and mob (four Weasels), without a campfire or seats, so no campsite is laid, and five scatter layers:

## Terrain layers and drawing order

Every grid's ground layers follow one project-wide draw order, the terrain style's layer order (`TerrainStyle.LayerOrder`, see [TerrainTypes.md](TerrainTypes.md#decisions)): grass, dry grass, meadow, tall grass, snow, dirt, sand, mud, ice, gravel, plaza, stone, shallows, water, ocean, lava, planks today, with the planned materials to go in at their places. Each generator lists only the layers its areas use, as a subsequence of that order, so a grid pays texture memory only for its own layers, and a pair of materials is drawn the same way up on every grid.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grass | `GrassTilemap` | `Tile_Grass` | `Grass` | `Terrain_Grass` | the Clearing, the Village Forest, the Lakeside and the Coast, as their ground |
| Weeds | `WeedsTilemap` | `Tile_Weeds` | `Weeds` | `Terrain_Weeds` | the Clearing, the Village Forest, the Lakeside and the Coast (patches on their grass, Phase 12) |

So the Clearing lists grass, dirt, gravel and stone, the Village Forest grass, meadow, tall grass, dirt, gravel, plaza and stone, the Lakeside grass, dry grass, meadow, tall grass, sand, mud, gravel, stone, shallows, water and planks, the Snowfield snow, dirt, ice, gravel, stone and water, the Coast grass, dirt, sand, gravel, stone and ocean, and the Lava Fields dirt, gravel, stone and lava, each with `TerrainStyle.asset` as its terrain style. A layer's index is its place in its grid's list, so it differs between grids: `AreaLayoutBuilder` and the generators reach a layer through the generator's own layer field, never a fixed index. Tiles are in `Assets/Tiles/Ground`, materials in `Assets/Settings/Terrain/Materials`. The grid's `TerrainBlendRenderer` draws the layers in one shader on the `Terrain` sorting layer at order 0, bottom first, so stone is drawn over gravel, the paths over mud and sand, water over everything where they meet and planks over the water; a layer's place is also its navigation priority, so the layer drawn on top of a cell decides it. Grass (the Snowfield's snow, the Lava Fields' dirt) is painted under the paths too: lower layers continue under upper ones, and the ground over nothing is the map's edge. Walls sit at order 10 on every grid, so they draw over the ground. To paint a material a grid has no layer for, pick its tile and press the palette's "Add the <material> layer" button, or add the layer to the generator and run Convert Ground.

`TerrainMovement_Default` has no rules for dry grass, the meadow, tall grass, dirt, sand, ice, gravel, plaza, stone or planks, so they are walkable at the default cost of 10, the same as grass. Snow costs 15, so paths take a trodden trail through it when the detour is short. Making roads cheaper would make units prefer them, but it would also lower the profile's minimum cost, which the A* heuristic uses as its step cost, and it would stop click-to-move from taking its straight-line shortcut across grass. Clicks would then bend toward the road. Mud and the shallows cost 20, which leaves the minimum at 10: paths go around mud when the detour costs less, and the shortcut is not taken across it. Water, the ocean and lava are not walkable, and since water is drawn on top it decides its cells whatever lies under it, so paths go around a pond and a click on water walks to the nearest walkable cell. The ocean, drawn over everything under it, decides its cells the same way, so the Coast's sea and the map's edge beyond it cannot be walked onto. The Lakeside's shallow ponds are walked at 20, and its dock at 10 like grass: the planks are drawn over the water, so they decide the dock's cells. Raised ground is not a layer: a cell of the grid's `RaisedTilemap` lifts whatever ground is painted there, and its cliff, the line between raised and level cells, stops every step across it whatever lies there, but at a ramp's foot, so units go up and down only over a ramp, walked at the cost of the ground on it. The terrain types are there for later uses such as footsteps or speed.

## Multiplayer

Generation happens in the editor, and its result is saved in the scene, so every machine loads the same terrain and props, and nothing is generated or replicated at runtime; mobs are only spawn markers. The Scene Generator's scenes are scratch scenes: nothing plays them. A layout world generation writes into a world's chunks (WorldGeneration.md's Phase 2) is chunk scene content like hand-painted ground: its mobs are spawn points under each chunk grid's `GeneratedMobSpawns`, each with a `PersistentId` derived from the world, its region's key and its place in the layout, unique in the world and unchanged when the world is generated again, so a host's save keeps naming the same mobs. The host spawns them as network objects through each chunk's `ChunkMobSpawner`, as for any chunk, and clients and late joiners receive them per chunk.

## Adding a generator

1. Subclass `AreaGenerator` with `[CreateAssetMenu(menuName = "TopDownRPG/World/...")]`. List the terrain layers it paints in `TerrainLayers` (only the ones its areas use, bottom first, each with its tilemap name, plain tile, terrain type and material; at most 32, and better 16, since a renderer of more draws in its slower wide variant (TerrainLayers32.md), their materials in the order of the style's layer order) and fill one `AreaLayout` of those layers per call to `Generate(seed)`, reaching each layer through its own field. Give its asset a terrain style. Build it on an `AreaLayoutBuilder` for the grass, painting, reservations, scatter, mobs and reachability, and derive its scatter layers from `PropScatter`.
2. Keep it deterministic: one `System.Random` from the seed (the builder's), and no iteration over hash sets where order matters. Terrain added to an existing generator after its props and mobs goes on the builder's detail stream (`StartDetailStream`), so existing layouts keep their props and mobs.
3. Keep paths on terrain, the spawn open, footprints apart and every mob reachable from the spawn, and test those properties over many seeds as `AreaGenerationTests` does for the six generators. Its first layer is its ground, the layer the builder lays the outline in (`LayGround`) and every other one lies on; a ground that is not grass needs the area's `AreaTerrain` pointed at it. Add its asset path to the determinism, writer and layer list test cases.
4. Create its asset under `Assets/Settings/Generation`. The window lists every `AreaGenerator` asset.
5. For raised ground, give its asset the raised and ramp tiles (`Tile_Raised`, `Tile_Ramp`), fill the layout's `Raised` and `Ramps` (`AreaLayoutBuilder.LayPlateau` lays a plateau with a ramp), and check reachability around `RaisedGround.IsCliff`.
