# Architecture

Code map for orienting quickly in the project. One section per system; keep headings stable and update the section that matches the code you change. Conventions and rules live in [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md); this file describes what exists.

All C# types are in the global namespace (no `namespace` declarations). Paths below are relative to this file.

## Overview

A Unity 6 (URP 2D) top-down action RPG prototype. The player boots into a main menu, starts a new game, and is placed in an area scene (currently the "Clearing", a forest clearing made with the editor's scene generator) where they walk in 8 directions and swing a sword. Weasel mobs idle, patrol around their spawn, spot the player with line-of-sight checks, path around walls on a tilemap grid, crowd around the player without overlapping, and melee attack on a cooldown. Damage shows floating numbers and a HUD health bar. Mobs target whichever registered player they spot (nearest visible), so the code is ready for more than one player. A dead player respawns at the area's spawn point after a delay while a teammate is still alive; when every player is dead at once the current area restarts. A dev-only stress-test area spawns hundreds of mobs on a procedurally generated map to profile AI and pathfinding.

### Load hierarchy

```
Main (MainLifetimeScope)
├── MainMenu (MenuLifetimeScope, parent = Main)        -- while in the menu
└── Gameplay (GameplayLifetimeScope, parent = Main)    -- while in a session
    └── Area_* (AreaLifetimeScope, parent = Gameplay)  -- exactly one area at a time
```

### Boot sequence

1. Play (or a build) starts in `Main`. In the editor, [PlayModeBootstrapper](../Assets/Editor/Scenes/PlayModeBootstrapper.cs) forces `playModeStartScene = Main` (toggle: `Tools/TopDownRPG/Boot From Main`) and stores the scenes that were open via [EditorBootRequest](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Scenes/EditorBootRequest.cs) (`SessionState`). Test Runner sessions are detected by their `InitTestScene*` scene and left alone.
2. `MainLifetimeScope` builds; its entry point [BootFlow](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Boot/BootFlow.cs) (`IAsyncStartable`) runs.
3. Editor only: `BootFlow` consumes the boot request. If an area scene (known or any other scene, via `SceneDefinition.CreateTransient`) or Gameplay was open, it calls `GameFlow.StartNewGameAsync(area)`; if only MainMenu was open it shows the menu.
4. Otherwise `GameFlow.ShowMainMenuAsync()`.
5. Menu "New Game" -> `MainMenuController.StartNewGame()` -> `GameFlow.StartNewGameAsync()` (starting area + starting spawn id from `GameScenes`). "Continue" starts a new session in the saved area instead (see Save data). "Host Game" starts hosting first, then continues from the save when there is one or starts from the starting area otherwise; "Join Game" connects to the address in the field and then waits in the menu until the host announces its area, which starts the game (see Networking).

### GameFlow

[GameFlow](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Scenes/GameFlow.cs) (singleton in Main scope) owns all scene transitions. Uses [SceneLoader](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Scenes/SceneLoader.cs) (additive load/unload, `Resources.UnloadUnusedAssets`) and [ScreenFader](../Assets/Scripts/Core/UI/ScreenFader.cs).

- Every transition: reject if one is running -> `TransitionStarted` -> fade out -> work -> fade in -> `TransitionFinished`. Cancelled with `Application.exitCancellationToken`.
- `ShowMainMenuAsync`: unload area + gameplay, load MainMenu under Main.
- `StartNewGameAsync(area, spawnId)`: unload menu/area/gameplay, load Gameplay under Main, then load the area.
- `ChangeAreaAsync(area, spawnId)`: unload current area, load new area under the Gameplay scope. Nothing in gameplay calls it yet on the host (tests only); clients call it when the host announces an area change.
- `AreaLoading(AreaTransition)` event: raised by `StartNewGameAsync` (`NewSession` true) and `ChangeAreaAsync` right after the fade-out, before anything unloads. [AreaTransition](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Scenes/AreaTransition.cs): `Area`, `SpawnId`, `NewSession`.
- Area load: enqueues an [AreaEntryRequest](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Scenes/AreaEntryRequest.cs) into the area scope with `LifetimeScope.Enqueue`, parents via `LifetimeScope.EnqueueParent`, sets the area active, then resolves [IAreaEntry](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Scenes/IAreaEntry.cs) from the area scope and calls `Enter()`.
- State: `IsTransitioning`, `IsInMenu`, `IsInGame`, `CurrentArea`, `AreaScene`, `GameplayScene`.

### AreaLifetimeScope ([AreaLifetimeScope.cs](../Assets/Scripts/Composition/AreaLifetimeScope.cs))

- `AreaEntry` as `IAreaEntry`, with every `SpawnPoint` in the scene and the area's `NavigationGrid2D` (null when there is none). `AreaEntryRequest` comes from `GameFlow`'s enqueued registration.
- `AreaClientReady` entry point: on a client, tells the host it is ready once the area is up (after the build callbacks below registered the area's network prefabs).
- `AreaTerrain` (when the scene has one): registered as a component (so it is injected), plus the `AreaTerrainSync` entry point (resolvable as itself, with the area scene) and a build callback that calls its `RegisterNetworkPrefab`. Registered before the grid check, since it does not need a grid.
- `NavigationGrid2D`: first one found in the area scene via [SceneQuery](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Scenes/SceneQuery.cs). Without one, nothing below is registered, and an error is logged if the area has mob spawn points.
- `AreaMobSpawner` entry point, also resolvable as itself (with every `MobSpawnPoint` and the area scene as parameters), plus a build callback that calls `RegisterNetworkPrefabs`.
- Logs an error for every `MobController` placed directly in the area scene: mobs must come from spawn points. Other scene objects needing injection go in the scope's `autoInjectGameObjects` (the stress scene lists its `StressTest` object).

[AreaEntry](../Assets/Scripts/Composition/AreaEntry.cs): sets the Gameplay-scope `ActiveNavigationGrid` to the area's grid (empty for an area without one), finds the `SpawnPoint` whose id matches the request (falls back to the first one with a warning), records it in the Gameplay-scope `ActiveSpawnPoint` for respawns, teleports every registered player this machine moves (`PlayerController.SimulatesMovement`: all players offline, only the local one online) to its own slot at that spawn point (`SpawnPoint.GetSlotPosition`, in registry order) and snaps the camera. It is `IDisposable`: when the area scene unloads, its scope is destroyed, VContainer disposes it, and it clears `ActiveNavigationGrid` if that still points at its own grid. Scope disposal is the hook (rather than `GameFlow.AreaLoading`) because it fires on every unload path (area change, new game, return to menu), at the moment the grid is destroyed rather than before the fade-out, and on clients too, without the Gameplay scope subscribing to Core events.

## Networking

Netcode for GameObjects, host-and-play. See [Multiplayer.md](Multiplayer.md) for the plan; this section describes what exists. Players, mobs and health are replicated. The host runs all game logic (mob AI, damage, deaths, respawns); clients move their own player and mirror the rest.

| Type | Role |
|---|---|
| [NetworkSettings](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/NetworkSettings.cs) | ScriptableObject (`Assets/Data/NetworkSettings.asset`): default join address (`127.0.0.1`), host listen address (`0.0.0.0`), port (7777), client connect timeout (10 s), max players per session including the host (4, range 1–4). |
| [NetworkSession](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/NetworkSession.cs) | Main-scope singleton that owns the `NetworkManager`: instantiates it from `Assets/Prefabs/Network/NetworkManager.prefab` (`NetworkManager` + `UnityTransport`, scene management off, no player prefab) and destroys it on dispose, so it never outlives the Main scope despite Netcode moving it to `DontDestroyOnLoad`. `StartHost()`, `JoinAsync(address, timeout, token)` (connects, waits for the connection, shuts down and returns false on failure/timeout), `Shutdown()`, `RegisterPrefab`/`UnregisterPrefab`, `JoinRefusal` (the host's reason when it refused the last join, else null), `IsActive`/`IsHost`/`IsServer`/`IsConnectedClient`/`LocalClientId`, event `ConnectionLost` (local client stopped without `Shutdown` being called). Makes its `NetworkManager` the Netcode singleton before starting, since Netcode falls back to the singleton when spawning. Implements `IGameAuthority` (`IsAuthoritative` = server, or offline when not following a host: a client whose connection dropped stays non-authoritative until `Shutdown()`, which `NetworkSessionLifecycle` calls once it is back in the menu, so a stranded client never saves or runs the game on its own). Area announcements: the host's `AnnounceArea(scenePath, spawnId, newSession)` bumps `AreaEpoch`, clears every client's readiness and sends an `AreaAnnouncement` to connected clients; a client that connects later receives the current one on connection. Clients raise `AreaAnnounced` and adopt an announcement's epoch with `BeginAnnouncedArea` once they start loading it. Ready handshake: a client calls `NotifyReady()` once its area is up, sending its `AreaEpoch`; the host ignores reports for any other epoch, otherwise records it (`ReadyClients`, `IsClientReady`, cleared on disconnect), shows it every object spawned through the session, and raises `ClientReady`. `DespawnObjectsIn(scene)` (host) despawns every spawned object living in a scene. `HasPlayerObject(clientId)`. `SpawnPlayerObject(instance, owner)` and `Spawn(instance)` (server-owned) spawn with a visibility check so objects only reach ready clients, and destroy with their scene. Also implements `INetworkObjectSpawner`, and [ISessionStatus](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/ISessionStatus.cs) (`IsActive`, `IsHost`) for the in-game menu. A client stopping while the application quits or the editor leaves Play Mode is not reported as `ConnectionLost`. |
| [JoinApproval](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/JoinApproval.cs) | Static admission rule, free of Netcode types. `CreatePayload(version)` is the connection data a client sends (its `Application.version`); `Evaluate(payload, hostVersion, playerCount, maxPlayers)` returns null to approve, or a reason: another or missing version first, then a full party. `EncodeRefusal`/`TryDecodeRefusal` tag the reason so a client can tell it from Netcode's own disconnect text. |
| [AreaAnnouncement](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/AreaAnnouncement.cs) | Area message payload: `ScenePath`, `SpawnId`, `Epoch`, `NewSession`, with `Write`/`Read` over Netcode buffers. |
| [NetworkAreaSync](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/NetworkAreaSync.cs) | Main-scope entry point. Host: on `GameFlow.AreaLoading`, despawns the networked objects in the area scene (and the Gameplay scene for a new session) so clients drop their copies before anything unloads, then announces the area. Client: applies announcements through `GameFlow` (`StartNewGameAsync` for a new session or when not in a game, otherwise `ChangeAreaAsync`), keeping only the latest one while a transition runs; an area that is not in `GameScenes` logs an error and leaves the session. |
| [INetworkObjectSpawner](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/INetworkObjectSpawner.cs) | `IsActive`, `IsServer`, `RegisterPrefab` (with a target scene), `UnregisterPrefab`, `Spawn`: the slice of `NetworkSession` area code needs, so EditMode tests can fake it. |
| [IGameAuthority](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/IGameAuthority.cs) | `IsAuthoritative`: whether this machine decides game state. Implemented by `NetworkSession`; tests use a fixed fake. |
| [IClientReadiness](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/IClientReadiness.cs) | `IsClientReady(clientId)`: whether a client finished loading the current area (the host's own client always has). Implemented by `NetworkSession`; used by `NetworkStatusEffects` to hold a loading client's player. |
| [InjectingNetworkPrefabHandler](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/InjectingNetworkPrefabHandler.cs) | `INetworkPrefabInstanceHandler` that creates network prefab instances through an `IObjectResolver` so their `[Inject]` methods run, moves them into a target scene when one is given (players into Gameplay, mobs into their area, so a client's area change never destroys the other players), and destroys them on despawn. Netcode only calls it on clients; the host must create its instances with `resolver.Instantiate` itself before spawning. |
| [NetworkSessionLifecycle](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Network/NetworkSessionLifecycle.cs) | Main-scope entry point: shuts the session down whenever a transition ends in the menu, and returns to the menu (with a warning) when the connection is lost anywhere but the idle menu. A loss during a transition (a client still loading its first area, or mid area change) is remembered and acted on when that transition finishes, since `GameFlow` ignores a second transition. A loss in the idle menu (a join that was waiting for the host's announcement) just ends the session. |

- `Assets/DefaultNetworkPrefabs.asset` is Netcode's auto-maintained network prefab list, referenced by the NetworkManager prefab: the player, the Weasel, the stress-test mob and `AreaTerrainSync`.
- Runtime terrain edits: only the host (or an offline game) changes an area's editable tilemaps, through `AreaTerrain.TrySetCells`. Each changed cell is kept in a per-area list (last write per cell wins) that the host's `NetworkAreaTerrain` mirrors into a `NetworkList<TerrainEdit>`; a client applies the whole list when the object reaches it (after it is ready for the area, so late joiners catch up) and each change after that. Every machine's `NavigationGrid2D` follows the tile changes on its own. A client moves its own player, so between an edit and its arrival that player can still walk where the host just built a wall.
- Scene management is disabled: `GameFlow` keeps loading scenes on every machine. Revisit in Phase 5 of the multiplayer plan.
- Netcode sets `Application.runInBackground` while a `NetworkManager` exists.
- Connection approval is on in the NetworkManager prefab (`NetworkSession` logs an error if it is off). The host approves itself, and approves a client through `JoinApproval` against `Application.version` and `NetworkSettings.MaxPlayers`, counting connected clients plus clients approved this frame but not yet connected. A refused client gets the reason as its disconnect reason; `JoinAsync` exposes it as `JoinRefusal` and the menu shows it. Approval is part of Netcode's config hash, so every build that should connect must have it on. Bump the Version in Player Settings whenever a change breaks compatibility between builds.
- Join order on a client: connect, receive the host's area announcement, load Gameplay (its scope registers the player prefab in a build callback) and the area (registers its mob prefabs in a build callback), then `AreaClientReady` sends ready for that area's epoch; only then does the host show it the spawned players and mobs and spawn its player (once per connection: `PlayerSpawner` skips clients that already have one).
- Area change or restart on the host: fade out, despawn that scene's objects, announce, unload/load. Clients follow, and new-area objects only reach each client after it reports ready for the new epoch.
- Unity Transport timeouts on the NetworkManager prefab: heartbeat 500 ms, disconnect 10 s (down from the 30 s default). The disconnect timeout is how long a client waits before giving up on a host that vanished without a clean disconnect (a crash, or it dropping off the virtual LAN), and the same for the host about a client. 10 s still rides out short Wi-Fi or Tailscale hiccups; a heartbeat counts as traffic, so an idle but live connection never times out.
- The NetworkManager prefab carries a `NetworkSimulator` set to the `None` preset. It drives every transport in the process, so only one should be enabled (the in-process test clients remove theirs).

### Transform smoothing

Networked transforms use `LegacyLerp` position interpolation with unreliable deltas and the default buffer (`NetworkTransform.InterpolationBufferTickOffset` 0, tick rate 30). This was chosen by measurement, not by the package docs' suggestion: the explicit benchmark `NetworkSmoothnessPlayModeTests.Benchmark_RemotePlayerSmoothness_UnderStress` moves an in-process client's player for 2 s at a time under 150 ms latency, 20 ms jitter and 4% loss and records, per rendered frame of the host's copy, how far the speed strays from the owner's, the longest freeze and visible backward jumps. Results on this machine (6 runs per setting, run in both orders):

| Setting | Start lag | Mean speed error | Runs with a freeze over 150 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LegacyLerp`, unreliable deltas, offset 0 or 1 | ~390 ms | 22–26% | 0–2 of 6 |
| `Lerp`, unreliable deltas, offset 1 or 2 | ~380 ms | 57–63% | 0–2 of 6 |
| `SmoothDampening`, unreliable deltas | ~350 ms | ~100% | up to 3 of 6 |
| Any type with reliable deltas | 450–550 ms | 60–150%, with backward jumps for `Lerp`/`SmoothDampening` | frequent |

Unreliable deltas are the biggest win (a lost update is replaced by the next one instead of resent behind it). Rerun the benchmark by name after changing tick rate, speeds, movement code or transform settings.

Rerun after click-to-move replaced WASD (the client's player now walks on far-away move orders, with the area's mobs despawned): `LegacyLerp` unreliable offset 0 gave 385 ms start lag, 21% mean speed error and no stall over 150 ms in 6 runs; offset 1 26%; `Lerp` 59–67%; `SmoothDampening` ~106%; reliable deltas 76% with stalls in 5 of 6 runs. The ranking and the shipped setting are unchanged. The 3-run regression test is noisier than the 6-run benchmark (38% in the same session).

## Camera and sprite sorting

- [CameraFollow2D](../Assets/Scripts/Core/Camera/CameraFollow2D.cs) (Core, on Main Camera): `LateUpdate` SmoothDamp follow of the target's Rigidbody2D position, separate smooth times for moving/idle, can snap to the pixel grid (32 PPU; `snapToPixelGrid` is off in `Main`, see Known gaps). `SetTarget`, `SnapToTarget`.
- The Main Camera's Pixel Perfect Camera (32 assets PPU, reference resolution 640 x 360) uses no crop frame: it picks the largest whole zoom that fits the 640 x 360 reference and shows the rest of the screen as more of the world, instead of drawing the reference into a centred box with a black border. At 1280 x 720 and 1920 x 1080 that is exactly the reference view. With a crop frame it renders into an offscreen texture blitted into the box, and Unity draws the Game view's gizmos as if the camera filled the whole window, so the spawn points' gizmos landed up to about 1.6 times farther from the screen centre than the world under them. The Pixel Perfect Camera warns in the Game view when the window has an odd width or height.
- [YPositionSorter](../Assets/Scripts/Camera/YPositionSorter.cs) (Gameplay, `[ExecuteAlways]`): sets `sortingOrder = offset - round(y * multiplier)` on a `SortingGroup` or on all child renderers (keeping their relative offsets). On the player, Weasel and death-animation prefabs.
- Sorting layers, back to front: `Default`, `Terrain` (ground: the blended terrain's chunks at order 0, the walls' render tilemap at 10; the move marker and hover ring also use order 10), `Entities` (units and props, sorted by `YPositionSorter`; slashes one order in front of their owner, or behind when swinging up), `Overlay` (world-space bars that draw over every unit).
- The camera culls the `Obstacles` layer, so the collision tilemap itself is never drawn. It is the data tilemap of a DualGrid walls module (`walls_DualGridRuleTile`): the module's `RenderTilemap` child, on the Default layer and the Terrain sorting layer at order 10 (above the ground), draws the 32 px wall art, while the data tiles carry `Grid` colliders, so physics, navigation and the art all follow the same cells.

### Player orders

[PlayerOrders](../Assets/Scripts/Player/PlayerOrders.cs) runs on the machine that moves the player (the owner). It holds one current order, at most one paused order while a cast runs, and at most one buffered cast. Each tick: Stop; a move press (or a held button every `HoldReevaluateInterval`) issues an order; an attack-move press issues an attack move (after a move press on the same tick, so it wins); an ability press is handled; a buffered cast older than `CastBufferWindow` is dropped; then the current order runs. A held button only keeps steering a move or attack: unlike a press, it never replaces a CastWhenInRange approach or drops a buffered cast (the camera follows the player, so the cursor's world point changes at every re-evaluation). One `fresh` flag marks the order that has not started yet (so a move ignores the motor's stale arrival); it is set for new orders, for the order resumed after a cast, and for the paused order when a buffered cast fires, so a buffered `Continue` cast restarts the paused move instead of reading the stopped motor as arrived. The output's aim is zero whenever there is no aim, so a swing at a target on top of the player falls back to the facing.

| Order | Issued by | Runs | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idle | Stop, or an order ending | Nothing | Any new order |
| Move(point) | Press (or hold) on the ground, an ally, or a dead enemy | `MoveTo(point)` once; the arrival check is skipped on the tick it is issued, because the motor still reports the previous path | Motor reached its destination, or stalled for `StuckTimeout` |
| Attack(target) | Press (or hold) on a living enemy; re-issuing the same target keeps the chase as it is | In `AttackRange` (collider distance): `Stop` once, aim at the target, request a swing every tick (the weapon cooldown decides). Out of range: `MoveTo(target)` when the chase starts, every `RepathInterval`, or once the target moved `TargetMoveRepathDistance` from the last goal. Walls and distance never cancel it. | The target dies or is destroyed |
| AttackMove(point) | The attack-move key, at the cursor's point | Without a target, every tick it takes the living enemy within `AttackMoveAcquireRange` of the player (collider distance) that is closest to the point (`IUnitQueries.NearestEnemyInRange`); with none, it walks to the point like a Move. With a target it engages it exactly like Attack (chase, stop once in range, aim, swing). Taking a target or losing it restarts the movement (the order is fresh again), so after a kill it walks on at once, ignoring the motor's stale arrival, or takes the next enemy in range. Paused during a `Continue` cast it walks, takes targets and chases, but never swings. | Arrived at the point, or stalled for `StuckTimeout`, with nothing in range |
| CastWhenInRange(slot, target) | A `Unit` cast whose target is beyond the ability's range | Chases the target like Attack | In range: the cast starts (and this order is gone, so the cast resumes into Idle). Target lost: Idle, reported as `NoTarget`. Another order replaces it. |
| Casting(slot, aim) | A cast that started | Faces the aim (unless the ability targets `None`). `Stop`/`Ability` movement: the motor is stopped at the start. `Continue`: the paused order keeps running its movement (a paused attack chases but never swings). | After `CastTime` (instant casts end on the tick they start): a buffered cast fires if it is still valid, keeping the paused order paused; otherwise the paused order resumes as a fresh order (a move repaths, an attack re-chases). |

Ability presses (`PressAbility`):
1. The caster's `CanCast` fails (`EmptySlot`, `OnCooldown`, `Dead`), or crowd control blocks the ability (`Silenced`; `Rooted` for `CastMovement.Ability`): reported, nothing else happens. The same control check runs again whenever a cast is about to start (an approach in range, a buffered cast).
2. The aim is built from the command: `None` ignores it; `Direction` aims at the cursor; `Point` clamps the cursor to the range; `Unit` needs the unit under the cursor to be alive and accepted by the unit filter (`Enemy`, `Ally` (registered players, the caster included) or `Any`), otherwise `NoTarget`.
3. During a cast: a non-bufferable ability fails with `CastInProgress`; a bufferable one becomes the buffered cast with that aim, replacing (and reporting as `Cancelled`) an older one.
4. Otherwise the cast starts at once (`IAbilityCaster.TryCast`), pausing the current order, or turns into CastWhenInRange when a unit is out of range.

Rules: a new order replaces the current one, except during a cast, when it replaces the paused order instead; a click (not a hold) drops the buffered cast (`Cancelled`). Stop drops the buffered cast and, during a cast, the paused order; the started cast itself runs to its end, then the player is Idle. A buffered cast is re-checked when it fires (cooldown, a dead unit target: reported and dropped, and the paused order resumes); a buffered unit cast whose target moved out of range becomes CastWhenInRange, replacing the paused order. An empty command (gameplay input disabled during transitions) makes no new order and lets the current one run on. Crowd control arrives in `PlayerOrderContext.Controls`: when a stun starts the orders `Clear` and ask the motor to stop once, and while it lasts commands are ignored, ability presses report `Stunned` and nothing swings (the player is Idle afterwards); when a silence starts a running cast is cancelled (its cooldown stays spent, its effect never lands), the buffered cast is dropped (`Cancelled`) and the paused order resumes; a root changes nothing in the orders, since the motor holds still without stalling, so a move or chase waits for it to end. A cast whose cast time runs out completes (`IAbilityCaster.CompleteCast`); one that is stopped early is cancelled (`CancelCast`). `Clear` (death, teleports, becoming a remote copy) cancels a running cast, drops the buffered one and every order without a motor request; the controller stops the motor itself.

## Mob AI: state machine

States are plain classes deriving from [MobStateBase](../Assets/Scripts/AI/StateMachine/MobStateBase.cs), which exposes the brain's components and config. "Spots target" below means `HasDetectedTarget` and `PathAgent.CanReachWorldTarget(target)`.

| State | Enter | Tick transitions | FixedTick |
|---|---|---|---|
| [IdleState](../Assets/Scripts/AI/StateMachine/IdleState.cs) | Clear path, stop, roll idle duration | Spots target -> Chase; timer done -> Patrol | Stop |
| [PatrolRoamState](../Assets/Scripts/AI/StateMachine/PatrolRoamState.cs) | Pick reachable roam point, build path; fail -> Idle | Spots target -> Chase; arrived or stalled `stuckTimeout` -> Idle | Follow path |
| [ChaseState](../Assets/Scripts/AI/StateMachine/ChaseState.cs) | Clear path | Lost target -> Search if hidden within lose range, else Return; in attack range -> AttackRange; every `repathInterval` repath, and unreachable / partial / goal-adjusted -> Return | Wait (stop, face target) if an ally is directly ahead near the target (with release hysteresis), else follow path |
| [AttackRangeState](../Assets/Scripts/AI/StateMachine/AttackRangeState.cs) | Clear path, stop, fire `onAttackRangeEntered`, attack | Lost target -> Search/Return; beyond `attackStopDistance + attackExitBuffer` -> Chase; else face + `TryDealDamage` + attack animation | Stop |
| [SearchState](../Assets/Scripts/AI/StateMachine/SearchState.cs) | Strict path to last known position; fail -> Return | Spots target -> Chase; once arrived or stalled, count down `searchDuration` -> Return | Follow path |
| [ReturnToSpawnState](../Assets/Scripts/AI/StateMachine/ReturnToSpawnState.cs) | Resolve spawn cell (or nearest walkable), build path; fail -> Idle | Spots target -> Chase; in spawn cell, arrived or stalled -> Idle | Follow path |

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    Idle --> Patrol: idle timer
    Idle --> Chase: spots target
    Patrol --> Idle: arrived / stuck
    Patrol --> Chase: spots target
    Chase --> AttackRange: in range
    Chase --> Search: lost, hidden in range
    Chase --> Return: lost / unreachable
    AttackRange --> Chase: out of range
    AttackRange --> Search: lost, hidden in range
    AttackRange --> Return: lost
    Search --> Chase: spots target
    Search --> Return: search time over / no path
    Return --> Chase: spots target
    Return --> Idle: home / stuck
```

## Crowd separation

[MobSeparation2D](../Assets/Scripts/AI/Core/MobSeparation2D.cs), owned by each `MobController`:

- `Sense(position)` each fixed tick: `Physics2D.OverlapCircle` (non-allocating, `ContactFilter2D` on the mob's own layer, no triggers) within `separationRadius * SenseRadiusFactor`; push away from neighbours closer than `separationRadius`, weighted by overlap; exactly coincident mobs get opposite deterministic directions from instance ids.
- Velocity = push * `separationStrength`, clamped per axis so it never points into an unwalkable cell (look-ahead on the nav grid).
- `HasNeighborToward(target, maxDistance)`: used by `ChaseState` to wait behind allies within `crowdWaitDistance` of the target instead of pushing into them.
- The motor adds this steering velocity to the path velocity.

Config: `MobConfig` Crowd header (`separationRadius`, `separationStrength`, `crowdWaitDistance`).

## Navigation and pathfinding

| Type | Role |
|---|---|
| [NavigationGrid2D](../Assets/Scripts/AI/Navigation/NavigationGrid2D.cs) | Area-scene MonoBehaviour. `BuildGrid` (in `Awake`) collects cells from each terrain source's tilemap, tagged with the source's `TerrainType2D`, then removes every cell that has a tile on one of its `blockingTilemaps` (the collision tilemap), so a wall blocks all terrain layers, and every cell that a collider on its `obstacleLayers` overlaps (`Obstacles` in every area: the footprints of blocking props). Those come from one `Physics2D.OverlapArea` over the collected cells at build time, each collider's bounds shrunk by 0.01 so one ending on a cell edge leaves the next cell free; triggers and colliders on tilemaps are skipped, since blocking tilemaps already cover theirs. The obstacle cells are kept for the cell refreshes below, so a tile edit never unblocks a prop's cell; a prop moved, added or removed at runtime needs a `BuildGrid` (`ConfigureObstacleLayers` sets the mask in code). Sources on a dual-grid render tilemap are skipped with an error, and so are blocking tilemaps that are dual-grid render tilemaps. The cells are then baked into a flat array over `WalkableBounds` (`TryGetIndex`/`GetCell`). Each `TerrainMovementProfile2D` (and the null profile) gets a `CostField` on first use: per-cell traversal cost (0 = blocked; where terrain layers overlap, the highest `NavigationTerrainSource2D.Priority` decides and equal priorities combine as most restrictive walkability, highest cost; plus the profile's `EdgeCost` on cells touching a blocked one), diagonal cost, and 4-connected region, rebaked lazily when the grid's `Version` or the profile's `Version` changes. Path follower flow ([PathFollower2D](../Assets/Scripts/AI/Navigation/PathFollower2D.cs)): world goal -> cells -> `FindPath` into a reused buffer -> smooth (skip intermediate cells only when the straight line is traversable and no more expensive than the route) -> waypoints (last one is the exact goal unless partial; the first is dropped when already within the reach distance) -> `Tick` steers toward the next waypoint, using the arrival distance for the final one, and grows `StalledTime` while the body covers less than 20% of `moveSpeed`. A failed build clears the path and the goal cell. When the grid's `Version` has moved on since the path was built, the next `Tick` checks the remaining segments once (`TryGetLineCost` from the current cell through each waypoint) and, if one is blocked, rebuilds toward the same goal with the same `allowPartial`; with no route left the path clears and `Tick` returns zero. Changes that open a shorter route are left to the owner's own repaths. `CanReach` = goal walkable + same region as the nearest walkable cell to the start. `MobPathAgent2D` calls it with `Time.fixedDeltaTime` and the motor's speed.

Straight-line shortcut (`UseStraightLineShortcut`): when the Bresenham line from the start cell to the goal cell is traversable and its cost is no more than the octile heuristic (so no route can be cheaper), the follower skips A* and uses the exact goal as its only waypoint. It is off for mobs: on random grids about 9% of the queries where it applies gave different waypoints than A* plus smoothing (smoothing stops at the first intermediate cell it cannot see, and A* breaks ties between equal-cost routes differently from the line), so turning it on would change mob paths. It is meant for player clicks.

Costs: 10 per straight step by default, diagonals x1.4.

## World and areas

- Areas are separate scenes with a `Grid` holding ground layers (plain data tilemaps, only those of the materials the area uses, in the shared draw order of `TerrainStyle.LayerOrder`: grass first, then dry grass, meadow, tall grass, dirt, sand, mud, the path layers (gravel, plaza, stone), then shallows and water over them, and planks over everything, each with a `NavigationTerrainSource2D` whose priority is its draw order) drawn by a `TerrainBlendRenderer` on the `Grid` (see Blended terrain below), a `CollisionTilemap` on the `Obstacles` layer (a DualGrid walls data tilemap; paint it with the Terrain Brush, which is the Dual Grid Brush there, with the Dual Grid Brush itself, or through `AreaTerrain`), `NavigationGrid2D` (with the `CollisionTilemap` as its blocking tilemap), optionally an `AreaTerrain` for runtime edits, one or more [SpawnPoint](../Assets/Scripts/World/SpawnPoint.cs)s (string `spawnId`, `partySpacing`, `GetSlotPosition(index)`: slot 0 on the point, then alternating right/left, gizmo), [MobSpawnPoint](../Assets/Scripts/World/MobSpawnPoint.cs)s (mob prefab, gizmo) and an `AreaLifetimeScope`.
- [AreaMobSpawner](../Assets/Scripts/World/AreaMobSpawner.cs): area entry point over `INetworkObjectSpawner`. `Start` (offline or host): `SpawnAll` instantiates each spawn point's mob prefab through the area container at the point's position, moves it into the area scene, and when hosting spawns it as a network object; spawn points without a prefab log an error. On a client it spawns nothing; `RegisterNetworkPrefabs` instead registers each distinct mob prefab so the host's mobs arrive injected from this area's container and in this area's scene, and `Dispose` unregisters them. Mobs are never placed in area scenes directly; the stress-test spawner's mobs stay local (offline tool).
- Project layers include `Obstacles` (collision tilemaps; `MobConfig.obstacleLayerMask` uses it for line of sight), `Player` and `Enemy`. Separation only senses colliders on the mob's own layer. `Player` vs `Player` collisions are off in the 2D physics matrix, so players walk through each other; everything else collides.
- Blended terrain ([Assets/Scripts/World/Terrain](../Assets/Scripts/World/Terrain), shaders in [Assets/Shaders](../Assets/Shaders), spec and phases in [TerrainBlending.md](TerrainBlending.md)): one shader draws an area's ground layers at 32 px per unit, in one of two looks: crisp (designed rounded edges with rims, outlines, shadows and tufts) or soft (shapes blended from the cells' weights and the materials' heights), switched by a developer toggle on the renderer. Every area's ground is drawn this way, each area listing only the layers it uses, a subsequence of the style's layer order (Phase 2 of [TerrainTypes.md](TerrainTypes.md)): the Clearing's `Grid` has a `TerrainBlendRenderer` with `TerrainStyle.asset` over `GrassTilemap`, `DirtTilemap`, `GravelTilemap` and `StoneTilemap`, the Village Forest's over `GrassTilemap`, `MeadowTilemap`, `TallGrassTilemap`, `DirtTilemap`, `GravelTilemap`, `PlazaTilemap` and `StoneTilemap`, the Lakeside's over `GrassTilemap`, `DryGrassTilemap`, `MeadowTilemap`, `TallGrassTilemap`, `SandTilemap`, `MudTilemap`, `GravelTilemap`, `StoneTilemap`, `ShallowsTilemap`, `WaterTilemap` and `PlanksTilemap`, each drawing the material assets of those names, and the stress test's over its `GrassTilemap` only, all saved in the crisp look. Water and the shallows are animated (their glints, sparkles and the swell of their shore rims) in the shader, from each machine's own clock. The ground layers are plain data tilemaps (no `TilemapRenderer`) holding their material's plain tile (`Tile_Grass` and so on), written by the scene generator, the Terrain Brush, `AreaTerrain` and `StressTestMap`; the dual-grid ground (modules, rule tiles, 64 px tilesets and render tilemaps) went in Phase 4. The Terrain Brush, the Tile Palette's default, previews a stroke on these layers through the renderer (see Editor tools).

## Known gaps and oddities

Factual observations against CLAUDE.md; nothing here has been changed.

- **Inline comments** (CLAUDE.md allows only XML doc summaries): `IClock.cs`, `SceneDefinition.cs`, the editor scene tools (`PlayModeBootstrapper`, `SceneBuildValidator`, `SceneDefinitionEditor`, `SceneSetMenu`), `VContainerDiagnosticsBuildGuard`, and several test files.
- **`MobConfig` defaults `obstacleLayerMask`** to a hard-coded `1 << 8`. Its create-menu path is `AI/...` while other assets use `TopDownRPG/...` (same for `TerrainType2D`/`TerrainMovementProfile2D`).
- **Mob config assets lag the class**: `Mob_StressTest.asset` has no serialized values for `searchDuration` or the Crowd fields, so those run on the C# defaults until the asset is re-saved.
- **No pooling for slashes or death effects**: `SlashSpawner` and `EffectSpawner` instantiate, and `SwordSlashAttack`/`MobDeathAnimation` destroy themselves. Only damage popups are pooled.
- **Allocating calls per attack**: `SwordSlashAttack` uses `Physics2D.OverlapBoxAll` and `GetComponentsInChildren<Collider2D>` on every slash.
- **`?.`/`??` on Unity objects**: `FindArea(...) ?? CreateTransient(...)` (`BootFlow`).
- **Static mutable state**: `SceneQuery` shares a static root-object buffer. `UnityClock.Shared` is a static used as a fallback clock inside components.
- **Two classes in one file**: `SceneDefinitionEditor.cs` also defines `SceneDefinitionPathSync`.
- **No in-game area transitions**: `GameFlow.ChangeAreaAsync` exists and is tested, but nothing in gameplay (doors, triggers) calls it. `GameScenes.areas` holds the Clearing, the Village Forest and the Lakeside, and the last two can only be played by opening them and pressing Play.
- **A party wipe restarts the whole session**: it reloads Gameplay + area rather than resetting the area in place.
- **The status dev panel finds the Gameplay scope itself** (`FindAnyObjectByType`, cached) instead of being injected, since DevTools types cannot be registered by the Gameplay scope.
- **Unused or test-only API**: `TerrainType2D.TerrainId`; `YPositionSorter.SetSortingOrderOffset`/`SetSortingReferenceY`/`ClearSortingReferenceY`; `ScreenFader.IsOpaque`; `PlayerWeaponController.CurrentWeaponName` (tests only); `AbilityService.CastApplied`/`CastResolved` (tests only); `PlayerAbilities.CastEnded` (no listener yet); `PlayerClass.DisplayName` (nothing shows the class); `MobController.onAttackRangeEntered` has no listeners on the Weasel prefab; `AreaTerrain.TrySetCells` (tests and the editor's dev menu only: nothing in gameplay edits terrain yet).
- **Duplicated tuning**: `nearestCellSearchRadius` exists on both `NavigationGrid2D` (used when no radius is passed, e.g. inside A*) and `MobConfig`. `MobMotor2D` speed/acceleration, `MeleeDamageDealer` damage/interval and `SwordSlashAttack.damageAmount` are serialized but overwritten at runtime by `MobConfig`/`PlayerWeapon`.
- **Magic numbers**: `MobDeathAnimation` scales the effect by a hard-coded `1.2`; the `StressTestSpawner` tooltip hard-codes "Detection radius is 6".
- **TMP's dynamic fallback font changes at runtime**: a glyph missing from the static `LiberationSans SDF` atlas is added to `LiberationSans SDF - Fallback`, which is package behaviour; in the editor that dirties the fallback asset. `Clear Dynamic Data On Build` empties it again on builds and when the editor closes, so the fallback is committed in that empty state. Keep UI text inside the static atlas's character set; if the fallback shows a diff with glyphs in it, find the text that used a character outside the atlas.
- **Editor asset writes on load**: `PlayerSlashPrefabBootstrap` can regenerate `SwordSlash.prefab` and rewrite `Sword.asset` from an `[InitializeOnLoad]` delay call.
- **Unit picks ignore the ability's filter**: `PointerTargetPicker` returns the closest living unit of either team, so an ally-only cast on an ally overlapping an enemy that is closer to the cursor fails with `NoTarget`.
- **Client status timers can read short after a host transition**: a client derives remaining time from the server time elapsed since the host last wrote the list, but the host's game clock (and so its statuses) stood still during its own transition while server time ran on. The host's removal is still authoritative; only the shown countdown is off until the next change rewrites the list.
- **Status icons are placeholders**: no status has an icon sprite, so the HUD shows two-letter abbreviations on coloured squares and the world icons are plain coloured squares; every visual is the same tinted ring.
- **The generated areas have no walls**: the Clearing's and the Village Forest's collision tilemaps are empty, and their edges are only missing ground behind a ring of trees whose footprints leave gaps, so nothing physical stops a body at the edge; paths keep players and mobs inside, and the default profile's edge cost keeps routes a cell in from the edge where there is room. `PlayerControlsPlayModeTests` builds its own wall at runtime.
- **Dual-grid leftovers from upstream**: `DualGridRuleTile`'s create-menu path is `Scriptable Objects/DualGridRuleTile` rather than `TopDownRPG/...`. `DualGridRuleTile.DataTile` logs an error and throws when the data tile sub-asset is missing.
- **Units sort by their sprite's centre, not their feet**: the player's and the Weasel's `YPositionSorter` sit on their `Visuals`, at the centre of their sprites: 0.5 above the feet for the player, 0.72 for the Weasel. Props put theirs 0.5 above their base to match the player, so a Weasel standing level with a prop's base sorts as if 0.22 further back.
- **Runtime terrain edits are not saved**: they live in the area scene, so an area change, a party-wipe restart or Continue brings back the authored terrain. Path followers only react to edits that block their path; an edit that opens a shorter route is picked up at the owner's next repath.
- **The camera does not snap to the pixel grid**: `CameraFollow2D.snapToPixelGrid` is off in `Main`, as are the Pixel Perfect Camera's grid and pixel snapping. The camera moves by fractions of an art pixel, so 32 px art, the blended ground included, changes shape for a frame whenever the camera crosses the half-pixel phase of the art grid. Turning the snap on (tried in terrain blending Phase 1) makes the moving player shake: the camera then jumps by whole art pixels while the interpolated player moves smoothly, so the player wobbles by up to about 2.7 screen pixels against the screen. Snapping needs the sprites snapped too, or a camera that snaps only once it settles.
- **Saving after touching a prop's renderer writes its sorting order**: `YPositionSorter` sets each prop's sorting order in edit mode without recording it, so the scene file has none, but once anything marks a prop's renderer changed (`forceRenderingOff`, a script setter), the next save writes that prop's order as a prefab override. `TerrainBlendProbe` clears the mark it leaves; other tools should too, or reopen the scene before saving.
- **The soft terrain look compiles on first use in the editor**: its shader variant compiles in the background the first time a renderer switches to soft in a session, so the ground draws nothing for a few frames. `TerrainBlendProbe` compiles variants synchronously, so its checks are unaffected.
- **A flood fill on a ground layer shows no preview**: the Terrain Brush keeps the Dual Grid Brush's flood fill, which from an empty cell outside a shape fills up to the tilemap's bounds, and previews nothing there (see TerrainBlending.md's Phase 3 notes). Undo it if it filled more than meant.
- **A ground tile is found by its material's name**: `TerrainTileWriter` names the tile and the swatch after the material, and only serves `Assets/Settings/Terrain/Materials`, so after a material is renamed, Write Ground Tiles makes a new tile and swatch, while the palette keeps the old tile and Generate no longer refreshes its swatch. Rename the tile and swatch along with the material.
- **The renderer does not check the shared layer order**: `TerrainBlendRenderer` draws its layers as listed, so layers reordered by hand in its inspector, or a material missing from `TerrainStyle.LayerOrder`, draw pairs the other way up from other areas. Only the scene generator's writer and Convert Ground enforce the order; running Convert Ground puts an area back in it.
- **An area gains a ground layer only through its generator**: each area lists only the layers it uses, and there is no command to add one layer by hand. To paint a material an area lacks, add its layer to the area's generator and run Convert Ground (the Terrain Brush says so in the Tile Palette), or add a tilemap and a renderer entry by hand at its place in the layer order.
- **Water blocks only through navigation**: it has no collider, so nothing physical stops a body there. Bodies move only along paths today, which go around it; a knockback or a dash would need a collider on a layer that does not block line of sight.
- **A layer that turns to its runs picks a way per cell**: the planks turn their boards a quarter in a cell where the layer runs further left and right than up and down, counting 3 cells each way, so at a junction, in a wide platform or at the end of a boardwalk wider than 3 cells the boards may lie either way, one cell at a time, and a painter cannot choose (TerrainTypes.md's Phase 5 notes).
- **A layer beside a lower one of another look shows it under its corners**: each layer's shape counts the cells of every layer above it, since lower layers are meant to go on under upper ones (a bridge, the planks, excepted: the layers under it count its cells only where they are painted there). Where an upper layer lies beside a lower one instead (mud or a road next to dry grass, with only grass under them), the lower one shows under the upper one's rounded corners where the two meet a third material: a notch in crisp, a spike in soft. Paint the lower layer under the upper one where they meet, or keep a cell between them, as the Lakeside's dry grass does from mud and paths, and its shallow ponds from every other look (TerrainTypes.md's Phase 3 and Phase 4 notes).
- **Diagonal paths step from cell to cell**: the path layers are filled cell by cell, so a road that wanders north or south climbs in cell steps. The crisp look's corner rounding turns the steps into a wavy band, but a diagonal never runs straight there (the soft look draws straight diagonals). The Clearing generator's `roadMeander` trades variety against steps.
- **Walls leave a gap at the ground's corners**: where a wall runs along the ground's edge, as around the stress test's border, the grass's rounded outer corner (about 14 px) and the walls' fillet (6.2 px) leave a few pixels without ground at each inner corner, where the camera's background shows. The 64 px tiles left the same gap.
- **Folder naming**: `YPositionSorter` lives under `Assets/Scripts/Camera` although it handles sprite sorting; the camera follow script is in `Core/Camera`.
