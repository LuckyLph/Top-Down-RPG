# Lighting

Plan for lighting the game: spells that light the world around them, and a day and night with a sun that moves and shadows that follow it, in light that keeps the 32 px art crisp. [Architecture.md](Architecture.md) describes what exists today. [TerrainBlending.md](TerrainBlending.md) is the ground's renderer, [Animation.md](Animation.md) and [PropGeneration.md](PropGeneration.md) make the sprites this lights. Tick off the phase checklist as steps land, and move each settled behaviour into Architecture.md as its code arrives.

Every rule in [Multiplayer.md](Multiplayer.md#rules-for-new-code-starting-now) applies. Multiplayer is checked by the PlayMode tests that run in the editor (an in-process host and client); checking by hand with Multiplayer Play Mode or a second machine is out of scope here and comes with a later feature of its own ([Roadmap.md](Roadmap.md)). Lighting is drawn by each machine on its own. The time of day is shared, so every player sees the same sky, but nothing about it is sent each frame, and nothing in the game's rules reads it. A player's lantern is the one thing sent: whether it burns, which its owner decides.

## Where we start

- **Lights.** Each world's root scene holds one Global Light 2D under its `WorldLighting`, white at 0.85, on the `Default`, `Terrain`, `Entities` and `Overlay` sorting layers, with normal maps off and no shadow casters anywhere. No other light exists. `LocalWorldPresentation` shows the lights of the world on screen and hides the others', since URP allows one global light per sorting layer.
- **The 2D renderer** (`Assets/Settings/Renderer2D.asset`): light textures at half the screen's resolution (the screen's since LightingGaps.md's Phase 4), HDR emulation scale 1, the four default blend styles, no renderer features. The light textures are `B10G11R11` floats where the platform has them, so they hold light above 1; a lit sprite's colour is `_HDREmulationScale * (albedo * modulate + additive)`, clamped only by the camera's target. Post-processing is off on the camera.
- **Lit shaders.** The terrain is a Shader Graph on the Sprite Lit target (`TerrainBlend.shadergraph`, a thin wrapper around `TerrainBlend_float`), lit after the graph by URP. Units draw with our hand-written `SpriteLitFlash.shader`, props with URP's `Sprite-Lit-Default`, the impact with `Sprite-Unlit-Default`. URP 17.4 also has the Sprite Custom Lit target, whose 2D Light Texture node hands a graph the light texture of a blend style to sample itself.
- **The light is painted in.** Everything is shaded from the top left, (-0.6, 0.8) on screen: the rig's four-colour ramps, the props' ramps, the terrain's chips, cobbles and raised ground (`TB_LIGHT`, a constant in `TerrainBlendInputs.hlsl`), and the cliffs' cast shadow.
- **Shadows.** A prop has a `Shadow` child drawing a baked sprite on the `Terrain` sorting layer: a hard ellipse of black at 35 % under the crown or body, shifted a little east, away from the light. Units have none. Raised ground casts a shadow below its cliff in the terrain shader (`TBCliffShadow`).
- **Casts.** A class cast's light (a glow at the casting hand, a star on the release, a ring and sparks) is baked into the cast's frames in its motion's colours: frost blue for Bolt, green for Mend, gold for Smite. Effects are pooled (`EffectPool`).
- **What later steps can reuse.** The rig keeps a depth and a surface normal for every pixel it draws, and so does `ArtCanvas` for props. Each terrain material has a height texture, and a cell field gives every pixel its offset to its chip's or cobble's centre. Shadow Caster 2D takes its shape from a Collider2D, a SpriteRenderer, a SpriteShape or a SpriteSkin.
- **The camera** is not snapped to the art grid (Architecture.md's Known gaps), so the screen-space light textures move against the world by fractions of an art pixel.

## Goals

- Light that keeps the art crisp: one value per art pixel, changing only from one whole art pixel to the next, never in gradients across an art pixel.
- Spells light the world: the ground, props and units around a cast, a bolt in flight and an impact, on every machine.
- A day and night: the sky's colour and brightness change over the day, the sun rises in the east and sets in the west, and the shadows of props, units and cliffs swing and lengthen with it. Night is dark enough for spell light to matter.
- Under the painted light, at the afternoon's sun, the game looks exactly as it does today.
- The same time of day for every player of a session, and after a save is loaded.
- Campfires, lamps and lava light the night around them, and their flames and glass glow.
- More lights that are not spells: torches, braziers and lamps along the roads, embers in the charred props, flames that move (Phase 6), and a lantern a player carries (Phase 9).
- Light that knows the world's shape: walls and props stopping a light (Phase 7), shadows falling down and onto cliffs, and relief under local light (Phase 11).
- A livelier sky: clouds drifting over the land and a sky of each region's own (Phase 8), and lights shimmering on water (Phase 10).

## Non-goals (for now)

- Darkness in the game's rules: sight, stealth, spawns or anything else that reads the light.
- Repainting the art unlit so the sun can light it. The painted shading stays (Decisions).
- URP's normals pass and its normal maps (Decisions: Pipeline). Relief (Phase 11) does without them: our shaders read where a light comes from in the light texture, and the props' normals are sheets of our own.
- Weather (Later).
- A player option for any of it.

## Decisions

The rules for crisp light, the painted light and the time of day were chosen with the user before Phase 0 (2026-10-30), on mockups composed outside the engine. Phase 0's previews in the engine then dropped the steps the mockups had, kept URP's lights over a light list of our own, and chose how sun shadows are drawn, with the user. The rest are proposals, confirmed as their phases land.

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Pipeline | URP's 2D lights for every light, combined by our own shaders: the terrain graph moves to the Sprite Custom Lit target and samples the light texture itself, and one hand-written lit sprite shader (`SpritePixelLit`, `SpriteLitFlash`'s successor) serves units, props, effects and the walls. No URP normals pass: it would draw the terrain's shader a second time. Chosen in Phase 0 over a list of lights the shaders compute in world space, which looks the same without steps and does not flicker with them, but gives up the editor's tooling, light shapes and Shadow Caster 2D; it stays the way back if steps or relief are ever wanted (Phase 0 notes). Shadow Caster 2D stops the lights since Phase 7, its shadows drawn by our own copy of URP's projected shadow shader (Light shadows). |
| Blend styles | Our shaders read only blend style 0's light texture (Multiply, URP's default for a new light), so every light uses that style: one light keyword (`USE_SHAPE_LIGHT_TYPE_0`) instead of URP's four, two light variants of a lit pass instead of sixteen. Chosen in Phase 1. |
| Crisp light | Light is sampled once per art pixel: the terrain at its snapped pixel, a sprite at the centre of the texel it draws, so a light's falloff changes only from one art pixel to the next. It is not banded into steps: chosen on Phase 0's previews over steps of 0.2 and 0.3 and a dithered version, which also flicker as the camera moves (Phase 0 notes). Since Phase 7 the light is read from the one texel of the light texture holding that centre rather than blended from four, so a light's shadow, decided per art pixel, reaches each art pixel whole and never crawls as the camera moves (Phase 7 notes). |
| Painted light | Stays. Every sprite and the ground keep their top-left shading, which reads as the afternoon sun. The sun's colour and the shadows tell the time of day. The ground's chips and cobbles keep it too (`TB_LIGHT`): their bevels are painted into the materials' textures and only the edges of stones cut along a rim are shaded in the shader, so turning those to the sun would set them against the painted ones; chosen with the user in Phase 4 (Phase 4 notes). Since Phase 11 the cobbles' and the plaza's bevels, their cut edges with them, turn to the sun and the local lights instead (Relief); gravel's chips and the planks keep their painted bevels. |
| Light model | `colour = albedo * (sky + sun * unshadowed + lights)`. The sky is the world's Global Light 2D. The sun is ours: its colour and direction come from the time of day, and it is taken away where a sun shadow falls, so shadows take the sky's colour and two shadows never darken each other. At the afternoon's sun, sky plus sun is today's 0.85 white. Built in Phase 4 as an outdoor world's global light holding the sky and the sun together, the light where no shadow falls, and the lit shaders taking the sun away where one does: the light textures hold 10- and 11-bit floats, so the sky stored there with the sun added in the shaders cannot draw the afternoon's pixels exactly (Phase 4 notes). Chosen with the user. |
| Sun shadows | A caster's own silhouette laid on the ground away from the sun: flipped at its base, stretched as the sun gets lower, leaned toward where it is, by a vertex shader from one global sun vector, so nothing runs per prop. Shadows lean a little toward the camera so they show instead of hiding behind their casters. A unit's comes from its current frame. They replace the props' baked ellipses, which went in Phase 4. A shadow is 0.5 per unit of its caster's height at noon, at most 2, leaning toward the camera by half its way across: chosen in Phase 4 over 0.35 and 3, whose noon shadows parted from their trunks and whose dawn and dusk shadows crossed the whole screen. Raised ground's cliffs cast into the same mask, from the shape of the ground under their top. Since Phase 11 shadows know the ground's height: a plateau's top stands the cliff's height above the level ground in front of it and is drawn that height up the screen, so a caster's shadow running off a top drops to the level ground below, the cliff's height times the sun's shadow vector further and that height lower on the screen, past the cliff's own shadow; a level caster's shadow stops at a plateau's side but for its parts higher than the cliff, which fall on the top that much nearer it and higher; and a cliff face, which faces away from the sun in this view (shadows always lean toward the camera), takes no caster's shadow, only the clouds'. Units and props read theirs the same way. Chosen with the user on Phase 11's previews over today's shadows lying flat across the faces and tops. |
| Shadow mask | The silhouettes are drawn into a sun shadow mask of one texel per art pixel, aligned to the world's art grid, so it never moves against the ground as the camera does; overlapping shadows are one shadow. A 2D renderer feature draws it before the frame (`RenderPassEvent2D.BeforeRendering`) from the casters' own sprite renderers with the caster material, over the view and a margin. The ground reads it at each art pixel; a sprite reads it two art pixels toward the sun from its feet, past the start of its own shadow, so a unit standing in another caster's shadow darkens whole and no caster darkens itself. Static casters are redrawn when the sun has moved about an art pixel at the tip of the longest shadow (every few seconds over a 20-minute day), units every frame. Chosen in Phase 0 over shadows multiplied onto the ground through the stencil, which cannot darken units. A lit sprite that casts nothing (the walls' tilemaps, the markers and rings, the auras, the arrow, the puff) reads the mask at each art pixel it draws, as the ground does: a tilemap's origin is not its feet. Since Phase 11 the mask keeps apart the shadows on level ground, on raised ground's tops, of level casters' parts higher than a cliff and of the clouds, with the ground beside it (Sun shadows). |
| Sun path | Rises in the east, passes high and sets in the west: long shadows to the lower left at dawn, short ones under the casters at noon, long ones to the lower right at dusk. The painted light is the afternoon. At night there is no sun, and the sky is a dark blue. |
| Clouds | Their shadows drift over the ground by day: the sun shadow feature draws them into the sun shadow mask every frame, after the casters, each texel keeping the most shadow of either (`SunShadowClouds.shader`), so a cloud's shadow takes the sky's colour like any other, never darkens another shadow, darkens a unit or prop standing under it whole, and stays on the art grid. Their field is four octaves of gradient noise, the first 14 units across, repeating every 16 of those (224 units); a cloud lies wherever it rises above the level that leaves the cover asked for under cloud, its edge fading over about a cell (±0.03 of the noise), and takes 0.75 of the sun away. They drift with a wind of (0.6, -0.15) units a second of the world clock, a cloud crossing the screen in about half a minute, so every machine of a session sees the same clouds and nothing is sent. Their cover comes and goes: each spell of 240 s of the world clock has a level of its own, hashed from its number, and the cover eases from one spell's to the next, between none and 45 %, times the region skies' (below). At night and indoors there are none, since the sun casts nothing. Chosen with the user on Phase 8's previews over clouds 8 and 24 units across, a hard edge, shades of 0.5 and 1 (under which the trees' shadows vanish), and a steady cover. |
| Time of day | Cosmetic and shared: the time of day is a start time plus the cycles the world clock (the game time played on the save) has run, so it is saved with the game as the world clock is, with no save format change. The host sends its world clock and the network time it read it at to each joining player, and again every minute against drift; each machine counts on from there on the network time. A day lasts 20 minutes and a night 5, in a settings asset. Chosen in Phase 3 over a time of day saved on its own, which only skipping time (sleeping) would need. |
| Worlds | A lighting profile per world: outdoor worlds follow the day, indoor ones (`SandboxInterior`) keep their authored sky and have no sun. `SandboxInterior`'s was today's 0.85 white until Phase 7 dimmed it to (0.28, 0.26, 0.32) at 1, under which its lights and their shadows show, chosen with the user on Phase 7's previews. Indoors there are no clouds and no region sky. |
| Region skies | Each region has a sky of its own (`RegionSky`, an asset its area generator names): a tint the day's sky and sun take, and how much of the day's cloud cover it has. The Clearing and the wilds keep the day's own; the Village Forest is green-gold (0.96, 1, 0.88), the Lakeside cool (0.93, 1, 1.05), the Snowfield cold (0.90, 0.96, 1.10) and cloudier (1.3), the Coast warm (1.05, 1.02, 0.94) and clearer (0.7), the Lava Fields a hazy orange (1.04, 0.86, 0.74) under smoke (1.3). At night only the Lava Fields' shows much. The world generator writes into each chunk where each sky reaches (`ChunkSky`): its weight every 4 cells, full from 8 cells inside its regions' outline, none from 8 outside, eased across it over those 16 cells, about 3 s at a run. Each machine blends the skies at its own local player's place, so the whole screen takes the sky of where the player stands, a shadow the tinted sky. The chunks name the sky assets rather than hold their colours, so a sky is retuned without writing the world again. Other worlds have no regions and keep the day's own sky, with clouds outdoors. Chosen with the user on Phase 8's previews over half and one and a half times these tints. |
| Spell lights | A Light 2D on what draws a spell's light, played on every machine from what already plays there, nothing networked. A cast's light is on the caster and follows the glow baked into the frame it shows: where the baker drew it, how strong it is drawn there (0.5, 0.75 and 1 over the gather, 1 on the release, 0.5 on the ring, 0.25 on the sparks) and in the motion's rim tone, at 1.2 and radius 3. An elemental impact (Fire, Frost, Lightning, Poison) lights in its spark's middle colour at 1.2 and radius 2, fading to nothing over the impact. Physical and True impacts and the weapon casts have none, and Bolt does not fly, so it has no flight light. Chosen in Phase 2. |
| Prop lights | Campfires and lamp posts are props the prop baker draws, a recipe kind of their own (`LightRecipe`: a campfire or a lamp post) in a `Lights` category. The baker marks where the light shines from and how strongly, as the character baker marks a cast's, and the prop writer puts a point Light 2D on the prefab (blend style 0; the `Default`, `Terrain` and `Entities` sorting layers; hard shadows at full since Phase 7, Light shadows), disabled, with a `PropLight` holding its full strength and flicker: nothing runs per prop, and a light costs nothing until it burns. The light stands on the ground under its flame or lantern, so a lamp's pool lies round the post's foot where light from the lantern would fall, while the lantern shows by its glowing glass: chosen on Phase 5's previews over the light at the lantern, whose pool floated 1.5 cells up the screen from the foot. A campfire burns (255, 186, 104) at 1.2, radius 4, flickering by 0.12, paler than its flame's (250, 156, 44), which was compared with it and with (255, 214, 160): on grass all three read mostly as brighter green, and the middle one warms stone and the rogue without turning them orange. A lamp post burns (255, 204, 110) at 1, radius 4.5 (over 3.5, whose pools barely reached the street from where the generator stands the posts), flickering by 0.04. A chunk's scope finds its prop lights as it finds its fading props and hands them to one presenter. Chosen in Phase 5. Torches and braziers are kinds of it too, two variants each, each blocking a cell: a torch, a pole about 1.4 cells tall staked among three stones, with an iron collar and a wrapped head burning, in (255, 176, 96) at 1, radius 3.5, flickering by 0.15; a brazier, an iron bowl about a cell tall on three legs, heaped with glowing coals and burning, in (255, 186, 104) at 1.2, radius 4.5, flickering by 0.1. Chosen in Phase 6. |
| Where they stand | A campfire with seats from the region's own stumps at the campsites of the Clearing, the Snowfield, the Coast and the Lava Fields; lamp posts along the Village Forest's street, laid from the detail stream so nothing laid before them moves, and one at each waystone's plaza. The roads stayed dark. The Overworld is written again, a new build version. Chosen with the user before Phase 5. Phase 6 adds, laid by the generators after everything else, so nothing laid before them moves: a pair of torches at each of the Overworld's gates, either side of its road; lamp posts along its stone roads, 12 cells apart, only within 30 cells of a gate and 5 cells or more from every waystone and gate, alternately on either side of the road on the first cell beside it, so the roads between nearby regions are lit end to end while the middles of the long coast and west roads and every gravel path stay dark, for spells to matter; a brazier at either end of the Lava Fields' causeway, a brazier on the Coast's plateau as a beacon, a torch at the foot of the Lakeside's dock and one where the Snowfield's trail stops short of the ice; nothing new in the Village Forest, whose square waits for its fountain. Chosen with the user in Phase 6, over lamps every 12 or 16 cells along every stone road and braziers where the village street ends (one hid behind a tree and the spot read as random). The build version stays pre-alpha (CLAUDE.md). |
| Night lights | Prop lights and lava's glow burn by one night lights' share of the time of day, a value of each light key: none from sunrise through the evening (0.70), 0.7 at sunset (0.80), full from dusk (0.84) to before dawn (0.96), none again at sunrise, so the afternoon draws today's pixels and the lights come up while the sun's shadows are longest. At none they are disabled. One presenter writes them, only when the share moves by 1/512 or a flicker step passes. Indoor worlds burn them at full. Chosen in Phase 5. |
| Flicker | A burning prop light's strength steps 8 times a second (the rate of the terrain's animated materials) to a value hashed from the step and the light's place, within its flicker share, from the clock and allocating nothing. A flame's pixels flicker too: the baker gives each a flicker colour, a tone next to its own (the outer flame's the middle tone, the middle's the outer or the core's, the core's the middle), and at each step the sprite shader shows 36 % of them in it, picked by a hash of the texel and the step, which the presenter hands it (`_GlowFlicker`): chosen on Phase 5's previews over the light's flicker alone, which left the flame still. Each machine flickers on its own clock, as its water moves. The light stays crisp, since every shader reads it once per art pixel. Chosen in Phase 5. A campfire's, a torch's and a brazier's flame moves too: the baker draws it as a loop of six frames, its tongues swelling and swaying and its tallest shedding a lick of flame, and the sprite shader plays them in order at the flicker's steps (a 0.75 s loop), each prop starting on the frame a hash of the art pixel it stands on picks, so flames side by side move out of step, the pixels still flickering on top; the shader picks the frame from `_GlowFlicker` in its vertex stage, so nothing runs per prop, and a lamp post's wick stands still behind its glass. Chosen in Phase 6 over 4 and 8 frames and over frames picked at random each step, which looked jittery. |
| Glowing pixels | The baker's glowing materials (a flame, coals, a lantern's glass) are written as a glow beside the sheet (`Assets/Textures/Props/<name>_Glow.png`: each pixel's alpha how much it glows, its colour the one it flickers to), the sheet's secondary sprite texture `_GlowTex`, and `SpritePixelLit` draws a glowing pixel at no less than its own colour whatever the light: barely brighter by day, bright at night. A sprite without one draws as before. Chosen in Phase 5. A sheet with a glow stays out of the props' atlas, which warns and keeps a mostly empty page when only some of its sprites have a secondary texture: it lies in `Assets/Sprites/GlowingProps`, the light props' and, since Phase 6, the charred trees' and stumps', whose embers glow steady at three quarters, cracks in the bark thinning out up to 0.7 cells above the ground and patches in the cut ends, with no light of their own. Chosen in Phase 6 over a black glow for every sprite of the atlas (32 more textures and a second, mostly black page, while a moving flame's rows could never be packed anyway), over flickering embers, busy in a view of the Lava Fields' 70 charred props, and over embers as strong as first drawn or stronger. |
| Lava's glow | Ground of a glowing material (lava) lights what lies near it, rather than lights along every lava edge: the terrain renderer builds a glow map with its control map, as it builds the coast map, its skirt included so the glow runs on across chunk borders, 4 texels a cell, (1 - distance / reach) squared out to a reach of 2.5 cells, and draws it through one Sprite Light 2D over its own cells in (255, 130, 50) at 1 (over 0.7), which the night lights' share dims; lava then shines brighter at night than by day, which reads as glowing. Only a renderer with glowing ground builds one, at its build: it is not baked, since it costs a chunk's load little (Phase 5 notes). Chosen in Phase 5. |
| Light shadows | Walls and blocking props stop the prop lights (campfires, lamp posts, torches, braziers) through URP's Shadow Caster 2D, which takes a light's shadow out of the light texture every lit shader reads, so nothing else changes. A blocking prop casts from its footprint, the box of its collider, written on its `Visuals` by the prop writer so its own sprite stays lit, and onto the ground's sorting layers only (`Default` and `Terrain`): URP darkens a sprite by its screen pixels, so a crown standing in front of a bush's shadow took a dark patch and a unit in a trunk's thin shadow darkened only at its feet. The walls cast from their collider, onto the ground's sorting layers too since KnownGaps.md's Phase 7, and the units and props in their shadows read them at their feet as they read the props' (LightingGaps.md's Phase 2), so a unit behind a wall darkens whole, its head over the wall's top too; until then they cast onto every sorting layer, and a unit's head kept the light where it overlapped the wall's top, which the walls leave lit. A light's own prop casts nothing: its light stands inside it, and its caster blacked the light out. Shadows are hard and stay on the art grid: our copy of URP's projected shadow shader (`LightShadowProjected.shader`, set in URP's global settings) decides each shadow texel by the art pixel it lies in, and the lit shaders read the one light texel holding an art pixel's centre (Crisp light). A prop's caster sleeps until a burning light within reach wakes it (`PropLighting`), since every enabled caster runs a script each frame and URP caches and culls it each render. A view may hold 8 casting lights (about 0.3 to 0.6 ms a render); the Overworld shows at most 4. Spell lights and lava's glow cast nothing. Chosen with the user on Phase 7's previews over a box round the trunk or body (blocky, and wider than a rock's or stump's drawing) and the sprite (crowns shadowing most of a lamp's pool), over soft edges (URP's penumbra is not on the art grid and crawls again) and over props' shadows darkening sprites. |
| Players' light | None for its own sake. A faint warm light around each player at night was to stay only if the night previews showed the rogue lost in the dark; the rogue read clearly at night on grass and on the road without it, so the night stays dark for spells. Chosen on Phase 5's previews. Since Phase 9 every player carries a lantern (below), dim enough that spells still show beside it. |
| Lantern | Removed from the player on 2026-11-05, with its key, its light and its replicated state (Phase 9's last note); the baker's lantern, its Aseprite layers and the visuals' lantern layer stay for a character that carries one later. As decided in Phase 9: every player carries one, always: no item to find, since nothing in the game holds items yet. It hangs plumb from the left hand, swinging a little with the stride, or from a hook at the left hip while the clip's weapon takes that hand (the greatsword's grip, the bow); the casts keep it in the hand, Mend's and Smite's too, wherever the cast moves it. A brass bail, an iron pyramid roof with a knob, a box of glass glowing in the lamp posts' tone (255, 204, 110) between four iron corner bars, the wick showing through, and an iron base, about 7 by 10 art pixels on the 46 px rogue. Out, it is not drawn at all, so the day keeps today's pixels. Its light shines from the glass's middle, as a cast's does from its glow, whether or not the body hides it: in the glass's tone at 0.8 and radius 3.5, flickering by 0.04 at the prop lights' flicker steps, casting no shadow. It lights itself as the night lights' share in the world shown reaches its share at sunset (0.7), indoors at once, and goes out as they fade at sunrise; a key of its own (`Lantern`, L, rebindable) lights it or puts it out at any time, the choice holding until the next of those. The owner's machine decides it and writes it to an owner network variable, so every machine, a late joiner's included, shows it. Chosen with the user on Phase 9's previews over the hip (a pouch at this size, hidden facing east), held out (stiff at a run, and a second set of every clip's frames), putting it away while the hand is busy (the light blinking out with every swing of a night fight), holding it lower for the casts, strengths of 0.6, 0.8 at radius 3 and 1.0 (which washed Bolt's blue toward white beside the player), the campfire's tone and a paler one, its light on the ground under it (no different at this height), casting shadows (faint wedges behind trunks, mostly hidden by the crowns, for 0.04 to 0.05 ms a render per lantern and casters woken round every moving player), a key alone, the dusk alone, and an item a player finds. |
| Relief | The cobbles and the plaza (the ground's cut stones) and the props answer where each light comes from; the units, the effects, gravel's chips and the planks keep their painted light. Where a local light comes from is read from the light texture, without URP's normals pass: the way its light grows from 8 art pixels to either side (2 crawled as the camera moved, the light texture being half the screen's size), divided by how bright it is there, so a light shines from the side at the edge of its pool and from above at its middle. A stone's bevel, painted into its material lit from the top left, is taken away and drawn again from each light's own side, in that light's share of the light: the sky keeps the painted one, so the night away from the lamps draws today's pixels and the afternoon, whose sun shines from within a few degrees of there, within a few steps of them; the sun draws it from where the sun is, where no sun shadow falls; a local light from where it comes, at 3 times the painted bevel where it shines from the side and not at all from straight above. A prop lights by its normals, which the prop baker writes beside its sheet: by a local light as much more as it faces where that light comes from, up to twice that light, and as much less as it faces away, to none of it; the sky and the sun keep its painted light. Each machine draws it on its own; nothing is sent. Chosen with the user on Phase 11's previews over rounded stones (domes from the cell field) at 0.5, 1 and 2 under a lamp, the painted bevel at its own strength there (barely showing), every bevel drawn from the sun's side alone by day (the afternoon changing and the night flat), relief on the characters (marginal at their size), on gravel's chips (their shadows are painted into the chips' own pixels) and from the materials' heights (no visible change), and props at twice the strength. |
| Reflections | Water reflects the lights: the water and the sea fully and the shallows at half, by a reflection each ground material holds (`TerrainMaterial.Reflection`), nothing else. A light's reflection is a glitter path down the water below it on the screen, toward the camera, as a reflection falls in this view, never above it: each pixel looks up the screen, from 0.2 to 8 units, for the brightest light but the sky's, fading to 0.3 of it at the far end, and reflects it only where it grows toward the light, sharpened to the light's bright core, in its own hue. The path is broken into dashes of 4 art pixels, 30 % of them hidden, changing 4 times a second, and each row of art pixels sways sideways by a whole art pixel at the water's 8 frames a second, so it ripples gently and stays on the art grid. Glints, highlights and surf catch the light falling on them, so the sea, which lies north of every light on the Overworld and so never shows a glitter path there, still lights up its surf by a lantern or campfire on the beach. Every light reflects, spells and lanterns included: leaving spells out would need a light texture of their own. By day water reflects at half (`DayNightSettings.DayReflection`), easing to full by the night lights' share; indoors always fully. Each machine draws it on its own; nothing is sent. - **The sun in the light.** The light textures are `B10G11R11` floats, which round 0.85 down to 0.84375 and the afternoon's sky (0.50, 0.52, 0.56) to (0.5, 0.516, 0.547), so the sky stored there with the sun (0.35, 0.33, 0.29) added in the shaders gives (0.85, 0.846, 0.837) where today's light gives 0.844: every pixel of the afternoon would change. So an outdoor world's global light keeps the sky and the sun together, as in Phase 3, and the lit shaders take the sun away where a shadow falls (`PixelLitShadowed`), which leaves the sky to within the textures' rounding. At the afternoon's sun on the forest's edge (with the first shadow numbers, 0.35 and 3), the render against today's with the ellipses hidden differs in 224,145 of 2,073,600 pixels, every one of them darker, inside a shadow; no other pixel changes (`PixelLightTests` holds it).
- **The mask.** `SunShadowFeature` finds casters by rendering layer, `Sun Shadow Props`, `Sun Shadow Units` and `Sun Shadow Cliffs`, so nothing is added to a prop. It culls them itself in `beginCameraRendering`, since casters off screen cast into the view and culling needs the render context: over the mask's area and the area moved toward the sun by the tallest caster's shadow (5 units tall). The area covers the view, the feet of casters up to 5 units below it, and a margin of 4 units, on whole art pixels, so the texels never move against the ground; the camera crosses the margin about every second at a run. The props' texture is drawn again when the view leaves the area, the tip of a 5-unit shadow moves an art pixel (about once a second at dawn and dusk, every few seconds at noon), or a scene loads or unloads; every frame the units are drawn over a copy of it.
- **Laying a sprite down.** A caster's vertices go from `(x, y)` to `(x + h * s.x, feet.y + h * s.y)`, `h` its height above its feet and `s` the shadow vector. The map is affine, so the sprite's own triangles and texture coordinates carry it exactly; the part below the feet (roots, a rock's front, the empty bottom of a unit's frame) would fold toward the sun, so the fragment discards it rather than clamping each vertex to the feet, which drifted the shadow off the trunk by as far as a triangle reached below them (up to 12 pixels times the shadow's length for a unit's frame).
- **Reading it.** The ground reads each art pixel's texel; a caster (a sprite on the props' or units' layer) reads one, in its vertex stage, two art pixels toward the sun from its feet; any other lit sprite reads each art pixel it draws, since a tilemap's origin is not its feet: the walls, the markers and rings, the auras, the arrow and the puff. Overlapping shadows are one shadow, and a cliff's and a tree's never darken each other.
- **The cliffs** cast into the same mask, drawn with the props: the terrain chunks of a grid built with raised ground are on `Sun Shadow Cliffs` (the style's `CliffShadowLayers`), and `SunShadowCliffs.shader` draws them with their own material, so it reads their elevation map and lookup texture. A level art pixel is shadowed when, looking toward the sun as far as the cliff's height times the shadow vector, it finds ground under raised ground's top: raised there, with raised ground the cliff's height above it, where the top is drawn. It steps by cells every 4 pixels and then pixel by pixel over a step that found raised cells. Ramps cast none, and raised ground takes none from its own cliffs. The shadow the terrain shader painted below the face (`TBCliffShadow`, the style's shadow alpha and height) went: at noon it doubled the sun's, and at night it was the only shadow left. The ramp's shade kept its column of the lookup texture, renamed `TB_SHARED_RAMP_COLUMN`.
- **Chips and cobbles keep the painted light.** Only the edges of stones cut along a rim are shaded in the shader (`TB_LIGHT`); every chip's and stone's own bevel is painted into its material's texture, lit from the top left. Turned to the sun, the cut edges changed 200 to 350 art pixels on the road at dawn and dusk against the painted ones beside them, and 217 even at the afternoon, while hardly showing. They stay painted (Phase 11: relief).
- **The ellipses retired.** `Prop.prefab` lost its `Shadow` child, and with it every prop variant and every placed prop: the chunks' prop instances override only their root's transform and name, so no scene changed. The baker draws no shadow and the sheets lost their shadow row, every slice keeping its ID; a recipe keeps only whether it casts (`CastsShadow`, off for `Stones_Small`, whose variants draw on the default rendering layer alone). The 128 variant prefabs dropped their overrides of the old child (`PrefabUtility.RemoveUnusedOverrides`), and the 32 recipes their shadow settings.

- **What it is.** `PixelRelief.hlsl` (where a local light comes from, `PixelLocalLightDirection`, the light texture less `_LightRelief.xyz`, and a sprite's relief, `PixelSpriteRelief`), `TerrainBlendRelief.hlsl` (a stone pixel's bevels, `TBStoneBevel` and the cut's from `TBInterior`, handed up through `TBLayer` with the layer's colour, and `TBReliefLight`), `TerrainMaterial.Relief` (the bevel settings `StoneCobblesRecipe` paints with, in the lookup texture's two relief columns) and the height texture's alpha (which way each stone pixel's bevel faces, `TerrainArt.FacingByte`, 0 on the mortar); the props' normals (`PropVariant.FrameNormals`, written by `PropWriter` as each sheet's secondary texture `_NormalTex`, linear, which the props' atlas packs into a page of its own, linear too); and `_LightRelief`, which `DayNightPresenter` writes (the light of the world shown, as for the reflections, and whether relief is drawn; `ReliefOverride`, the lighting panel's `H`). - **Why the textures keep the painted bevel.** The cobbles' and the plaza's albedo stays as it was, lit from the top left like every sprite's, and the shader takes that bevel away and draws it again from each light's side, rather than the materials being written flat: the afternoon, whose sun at its key shines 3 degrees from the painted side, then draws today's pixels within a few steps, nearly all of them within one (the spike's flat albedo left 2.6 % of them more than 4 steps off), and the editor's unlit views, the swatches, the prop recipes' previews and every tool that shows a material's albedo keep showing it as the game draws it at the afternoon. Only the height textures changed, in their alpha, which nothing read before. The bevel's direction is stored rather than rebuilt from the cell field, which points at a stone's centre pixel and not its seed, and marks the mortar, which the field's distance to the stone's edge, rounded to a sixteenth of a pixel, could not.
- **The light texture's rounding.** A local light counts only above 0.02 in its brightest channel: the light texture's 10- and 11-bit floats round the sky by up to 0.008, which would otherwise draw every stone as faintly lit.

- **How a shadow down a cliff meets the cliff's own.** A top's front edge stands the cliff's height above the level ground's, and the sun shines from behind the plateau, so the part of a shadow running off the edge lands on the level ground the cliff's height times the sun's shadow vector further than it would on the top, and the cliff's height lower on the screen: beyond the face and the band of the cliff's own shadow, which it meets, so the two read as one shadow with the tree's going on past it. The face between takes none: no sun ray reaches a face facing the camera while shadows lean toward it, from a caster on the top or below. A level caster's shadow can only reach a plateau from its sides or its back, where the walls are not drawn; the parts of the caster higher than the cliff clear them and fall on the top, the cliff's height times the shadow vector nearer the caster and that height higher on the screen than on the level ground, and the rest stop there, so of a tree one and a half times the cliff's height only a sliver of shadow reaches the top.
- **What it is.** The sun shadow mask holds four channels (Decisions: Shadow mask): red the shadows on level ground (the level casters', the cliffs' own and the clouds'), green those on tops (the casters standing on a top, and the clouds), blue those of level casters' parts higher than a cliff, alpha the clouds alone; beside it a ground texture, drawn with the props' shadows by the cliffs' shader's second pass over each raised chunk's quad, grown by the tallest caster's shadow and a shadow's drop, holds what the ground is (level, a face or a top) and the cliff's height near raised ground. A caster reads the ground under its feet in its vertex stage to know which channels it writes; every lit shader reads its shadow through `SunShadowAt`, by the ground there, so the ground, the units and the props agree.

## Open questions

- Whether band edges flicker as the camera moves, since the light textures are in screen space at half resolution and the camera is not snapped. Answered in Phase 0: they do with URP's light textures, and they do not with lights the shaders compute from a list in world space; without steps neither does.
- How the sun shadows reach the sprites: a mask drawn before the `Entities` layer (a renderer feature) that units sample at their feet, or shadows multiplied onto the ground with a stencil, which cannot darken units. Answered in Phase 0: the mask (Decisions: Shadow mask).
- Whether sampling at a sprite's texel centres holds for props packed in the sprite atlas. Answered in Phase 0: it does; the atlas-packed props lit and matched today's pixels.
- Why a sprite sharing one lit material with the atlas-packed props drew with the atlas's pixels in the spike (Phase 0 notes), before Phase 1 puts every sprite on one shader. Answered in Phase 1: only in the editor update after the sprite shader is imported, through the SRP Batcher; never in a build (Phase 1 notes).
- How bright a spell may be in daylight before the art's light colours burn to white. Answered in Phase 2: 1.2 burns nothing on the cobbles or the gravel; from 1.6 the brightest stones start to clip (Phase 2 notes).
- What a light costs while it is loaded but off screen (its `LateUpdate` and its cull each frame), which Phase 2 did not measure, and so how many a chunk may hold. Answered in Phase 5: about 0.3 to 0.5 µs a frame in the player loop and nothing in a render; the Overworld holds 16 prop lights, at most 5 in a chunk, so no cap is needed (Phase 5 notes). Phase 6's 72, at most 9 in a chunk and 4 in a view, still need none (Phase 6 notes).
- Whether glow masks, secondary textures of only some sprites, pack well in the props' Sprite Atlas, which warns about such a mismatch. Answered in Phase 5: they do not; the atlas warned and would keep a mostly empty page, so the light props' sheets stay out of it (Phase 5 notes), and since Phase 6 every sheet with a glow, the charred props' too (Decisions: Glowing pixels).
- How many lights a frame can afford, measured in a player. Answered in Phase 2: far more than play makes. In a Windows development player, 32 spell lights on screen add about 0.04 ms to a 1920 x 1080 frame and 256 under 1 ms, against a few at once in a fight (Phase 2 notes).
